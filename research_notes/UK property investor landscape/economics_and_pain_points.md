## 1. Costs and targets that apply to every strategy: profit targets, stamp duty, bridging and development finance, build costs, fees

### Takeaway
Lenders and viability guidance set **20% profit on GDV** as the benchmark for new-build and conversion schemes. That benchmark can drop to about 15% when units are pre-sold, and anything under 15% is a red flag. Flippers use rules of thumb: aim for **20% ROI** or a 20–25% gross margin at purchase. Since the stamp-duty surcharge on additional homes rose to **5%** (31 Oct 2024), and the nil-rate band fell to £125k (1 Apr 2025), stamp duty has become one of the largest costs on a flip. Bridging costs roughly **0.55–1.0% a month**, with the average around 0.82% in Q1 2026. Development finance costs about **0.65–0.90% a month** plus 1–2% arrangement fees. South East build costs run about **£2,200–£3,000/m²** for mid-range single-storey extensions and **£2,500–£3,000/m²** for an average self-build new home.

### Cited Findings

**Profit targets (lender and industry benchmarks)**
- The benchmark for residential development is a minimum **20% profit on GDV**. Some lenders accept **17–18%** for low-risk schemes with strong pre-sales or prime locations, and "below **15%** is a red flag for almost every lender". — [Construction Capital – Development Finance Profit Margins](https://constructioncapital.co.uk/guides/development-finance-profit-margins); [Construction Capital – Development Appraisal for Lenders](https://constructioncapital.co.uk/guides/development-appraisal-lenders)
- Pre-sold schemes may be approved with margins as low as **15% on GDV**, because most of the sales risk has gone. First-time developers are often asked to show higher margins. — [Construction Capital – Development Appraisal for Lenders](https://constructioncapital.co.uk/guides/development-appraisal-lenders)
- Residual land value = GDV − (land, build, finance, professional fees) − profit (**c.20% of GDV**). — [LandTech – GDV in property](https://land.tech/blog/gdv-in-property-what-it-means-and-how-to-calculate-it)
- The DLUHC Planning Practice Guidance on viability uses **17–20% profit on GDV** as the standard benchmark in viability assessments. Profit on cost is quoted as having a "typical industry minimum" of **25%**. Professional fees (architect, planning, engineers, QS, legal, marketing) typically run **8–12% of build cost**, and the standard contingency is **10% of build cost**. — [RapidQS – Development appraisal examples](https://rapidqs.co.uk/development-appraisal-examples-how-to-model-build-costs-and-profit/); [Assesr – Development appraisal guide](https://assesr.com/blog/development-appraisal-guide) (the search summary merged these two; the exact page for each number is not isolated)
- Standard residential new-build costs in 2026 are quoted as **£180–£250/sq ft** (same RapidQS/Assesr results as above). — [RapidQS](https://rapidqs.co.uk/development-appraisal-examples-how-to-model-build-costs-and-profit/)
- A local-authority CIL viability model for a "Small Scale Infill" scenario (2×2-bed, 2×3-bed and 1×4/5-bed houses) assumes **20% developer profit on GDV** for market housing. — [West Oxfordshire DC CIL viability appraisal spreadsheets, Jan 2020](https://westoxon.gov.uk/media/xnwompup/cil-viability-appraisal-spreadsheets-january-2020.pdf)

**Flipper rules of thumb (labelled as such)**
- *Rule of thumb:* aim for **20% ROI**. If you aim for 10% and misjudge costs, "you could easily find yourself making no money at all". — [Property Geek – How to flip](https://propertygeek.net/article/how-to-flip/) (search summary; possibly Sourced.co)
- *Rule of thumb:* "if you can't see at least a **20–25% gross margin** in the deal at purchase, walk away". Also: "every flip overruns by **10–30%** on time and budget", and you should budget at least **10%** of works for surprises. — [Property Accelerator – Flipping houses UK 2026](https://property-accelerator.co.uk/flipping-houses-uk/); [Lendlord – House flipping UK 2026](https://lendlord.io/house-flipping-uk-2026-costs-rules-and-how-landlords-profit) (merged summary)
- *Rule of thumb (imported from the US):* the **70% rule** says pay at most 70% of after-repair value (ARV) minus works, e.g. £200,000 × 0.70 − £30,000 = **£110,000**. — [PropAI Deals – Flip calculator](https://www.propaideals.co.uk/flip-calculator)
- An illustrative UK flip from a guide: buy at **£150,000**, spend **£30,000**, sell at **£220,000**, for a "profit" of **£40,000** before transaction and finance costs. — [Sourced.co – UK property flipping](https://sourced.co/blog/all-you-need-to-know-about-uk-property-flipping/)

**Market evidence on flip profits (Hamptons, Land Registry-based)**
- In 2025, **1.5%** of all transactions in England & Wales were flips (down from 2.0% in 2024). **10,570** homes were flipped, against 21,520 in 2016. The number was the lowest since 2012 and the share the lowest since 2013. — [The Intermediary, Apr 2026](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/04/share-of-flipped-homes-falls-to-decade-low-hamptons-finds/); [Hamptons – Lowest share of homes flipped in a decade](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/lowest-share-of-homes-flipped-in-a-decade)
- Average flip profit was **£16,390 in 2025**, against £36,500 in 2015 (−55%). **Sources conflict on whether this is gross or after stamp duty.** Mortgage Solutions' summary calls it "gross profit", excluding renovation costs ([Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/)). The Intermediary calls it "average post-Stamp Duty profits" ([The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/04/share-of-flipped-homes-falls-to-decade-low-hamptons-finds/)).
- Stamp duty took **71%** of the average gross flip profit in 2025. **73.3%** of 2025 flips made a gross profit, but only **58.7%** did after stamp duty. The North East did best, with average returns of **36.4%**. — [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/); [Landlord Today – "You can't make money from flipping any more", Apr 2026](https://www.landlordtoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/04/you-cant-make-money-from-flipping-any-more/)
- In Q1 2025 the average gross profit on a flip in England & Wales was **£22,000**, down from a peak of **£38,000** in 2022. — [Hamptons Flipping Index Q1 2025](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/flipping-index-q1-2025)
- Hamptons' definition of a flip (resale within about 12 months) means these figures exclude refurbishment costs and cover all quick resales, not just investor refurb flips. — [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/) (the "excludes renovation costs" point comes from the summary)

**Stamp duty (SDLT, England)**
- The surcharge on additional dwellings rose from 3% to **5%** on **31 October 2024**. It applies when the buyer owns another residential property at the end of the transaction day. It does not apply below **£40,000**, and it can be refunded if a main residence is replaced within 36 months. — [Price Bailey – SDLT 5% surcharge](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/blog/stamp-duty-additional-property/); [Connaught Law – SDLT surcharge 2026 rules](https://connaughtlaw.com/sdlt-additional-property-surcharge-guide/)
- From **1 April 2025** the nil-rate band fell from £250,000 to **£125,000**. Additional-dwelling buyers therefore pay **5%** on the first £125k and **7%** on £125,001–£250,000. — [Michelmores – SDLT changes 1 April 2025](https://www.michelmores.com/tax-insight/buyer-beware-stamp-duty-land-tax-is-changing-from-1-april-2025/); [Perrys Accountants](https://perrysaccountants.co.uk/news/changes-to-stamp-duty-land-tax-2025)

**Bridging finance (flips, BRRR purchase, auction buys)**
- About **£200m** of bridging was lent in Q1 2026 at an average monthly rate of **0.82%**. Most mainstream deals in 2026 are priced at **0.65–0.95%/month**. — 2026 broker rate guides from search ([Spark Finance](https://www.sparkfinance.co.uk/blog/bridging-loan-rates-uk-2026); [Fox Davidson](https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/finance-guide/bridging-loan-rates/); [Funding Agent](https://www.fundingagent.co.uk/post/bridging-loan-interest-rates)). The merged summary does not say which page carried the Q1 2026 figure; it looks like industry "Bridging Trends" data.
- Pricing by risk: at **≤65% LTV** with clean credit and a clear exit, from about **0.55–0.65%/month**. At **70% LTV**, mixed credit or an open bridge, **0.75–1.0%/month**. The overall range is **0.5–1.5%/month**. Arrangement, exit and valuation fees add **3–5%** to the total cost. — same broker guides as above ([Spark Finance](https://www.sparkfinance.co.uk/blog/bridging-loan-rates-uk-2026); [Westminster Finance](https://westminsterfinance.uk/how-much-do-bridging-loans-cost/))
- Refurbishment bridging: **light refurb from 0.60%/month**, typically **0.60–1.20%/month**. Light refurb is usually **0.20–0.40%/month cheaper** than heavy refurb. **Heavy refurb for first-time developers costs about 1.0–1.5%/month.** A typical structure advances **70%** of the purchase price on day one and funds **100% of works** in tranches, paid in arrears. — [Aura Capital – light refurb bridging](https://www.auracapital.co.uk/bridging-products/light-refurbishment-bridging-loans); [FD Commercial – refurbishment finance](https://www.fdcommercial.co.uk/finance-guide/property-refurbishment-finance/); [SME Bridging Finance](https://www.smebridgingfinance.co.uk/types/refurbishment-bridging-loans/) (merged summary)

**Development finance (small new-build, conversions, replacement dwellings)**
- 2026 rates for residential ground-up schemes by established developers are **0.65–0.90%/month**. Higher gearing, less experience or complex structures push this to **1.10%/month or more**. Arrangement fees are **1–2%** of the facility, plus monitoring surveyor, valuation, and a broker fee of **up to 1%**. — [Fox Davidson – development finance rates 2026](https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/finance-guide/development-finance-rates/); [Construction Capital – current rates](https://constructioncapital.co.uk/guides/current-development-finance-rates-uk); [ABC Finance – rates and fees](https://abcfinance.co.uk/development-finance/rates-and-fees/) (merged summary)
- Lenders typically advance **85–90% of costs**, subject to a cap on loan-to-GDV (LTGDV), so the developer funds at least **10–15%**. **First-time developers usually face about 85% LTC and 65% LTGDV caps**, higher rates and more monitoring. — same sources as above

**Build costs (South East / East, 2025–26)**
- Extensions: the UK average is **£1,200–£2,500/m²** in 2026, with London and the South East adding **20–40%**. Mid-range **single-storey extensions in the South East cost £2,200–£3,000/m²**. Single-storey generally runs **£1,800–£3,000/m²**, with Kent and the South East at the higher end. One guide puts London/SE single-storey at **£2,800–£4,500/m²**. — [HouseUp – extension cost 2026](https://www.houseup.co.uk/news/how-much-does-house-extension-cost); [BestBuilders – extension cost UK 2026](https://www.bestbuilders.co.uk/costs/house-extension-cost-uk); [DNDH Construction – extension cost per m²](https://dndhconstruction.co.uk/blog/house-extension-cost-per-m2.html); [GetEstimateAI – extension cost 2026](https://getestimateai.co.uk/articles/house-extension-cost-uk-2026) (merged summary; ranges differ between guides)
- New build / self-build: an average self-build in 2026 is likely to cost **£2,500–£3,000/m²**. The overall range runs from about **£1,800/m²** (basic, lower-cost regions) to **over £4,500/m²** (high-spec London). On 2025 BCIS-based figures, a house costing **£2,000/m²** at the national average would cost about **£2,600/m²** in Inner London and **£1,720/m²** in the North East. These figures exclude land, fees, planning, infrastructure and landscaping. — [Build It / self-build.co.uk – cost to build 2026](https://www.self-build.co.uk/what-does-it-cost-to-build-a-house/); [ProQuant Estimating – new build cost per m² 2026](https://www.proquantestimating.co.uk/new-build-house-cost-uk-what-to-expect-per-square-metre-in-2026/); [GDV Calculator – BCIS 2026](https://gdvcalculator.co.uk/cost-lines/build-cost-bcis-2026) (merged summary)

**Planning fees**
- New planning fees took effect **1 April 2025**, and fees are now **indexed each April to CPI** (September figure, capped at **10%**). The base fee for over 50 dwellings is **£31,385 plus £189** for each additional dwelling. Prior approval for commercial-to-residential rose from £125 to **£250 per dwelling**. — [Lichfields – new planning fees from 1 April 2025](https://lichfields.uk/blog/2025/january/17/new-planning-application-fees-from-1-april-2025); [GOV.UK – fees for planning applications](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/fees-for-planning-applications); [GOV.UK – annual indexation](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/planning-fees-annual-indexation-from-1-april)
- An HMO guide puts the fee for a change-of-use application in an Article 4 area at about **£578**. — [HMO Checker / renovat.co.uk (merged summary)](https://hmochecker.co.uk/hmo-conversion-costs/)

### Inferences
- **(calc) SDLT on an additional dwelling after April 2025**, using the cited 5%/7% bands and assuming the 5% surcharge on top of the standard 5% band above £250k (i.e. 10%): **£250k → £15,000** (6,250 + 8,750); **£300k → £20,000**; **£400k → £30,000**; **£500k → £40,000**. At typical South East/East flip prices, SDLT alone is **6–8% of the purchase price**. That matches Hamptons' finding that stamp duty takes most of the average gross flip profit.
- **(calc) Illustrative bridging cost on a £250k cosmetic flip**: a 70% LTV loan of £175k at 0.82%/month for 8 months is about **£11,500** interest. Add about 2% arrangement (about £3,500) plus valuation and legal fees, so roughly **£15–17k** of finance. SDLT (about £15k) plus finance (about £16k) is therefore about **£31k of friction before any works, agent (about 1–1.5%) or legal fees**. This is why the 20–25% gross-margin rule of thumb matters.
- Hamptons' falling flip volumes (10,570 in 2025) show the cosmetic/light flip is the segment most squeezed by the surcharge. Value-add that creates a *new asset* (extra bedroom, extra unit, planning) is less exposed, because the uplift is larger relative to SDLT.
- **(calc)** Converting £180–£250/sq ft gives about **£1,940–£2,690/m²**, which is consistent with the South East self-build range of £2,500–£3,000/m².

## 2. Strategy-by-strategy economics: margins/yields, costs, timelines, finance and risks

### Takeaway
Across strategies, the evidence points to a hierarchy. Cosmetic flips are structurally squeezed by stamp duty: the average England & Wales flip made about £16k in 2025 and roughly 4 in 10 lost money after stamp duty. Extension flips depend on buying well, because a bedroom adds about 10–13% of value while South East build costs are £2,200–£3,000/m². Conversions, HMOs and new-build schemes are appraised on 15–20%+ profit on GDV, or on yield (HMOs in northern cities at about 12%). South East standard buy-to-let grosses only about 5.6–6.1%, against 4.9–5.7% mortgage rates. BRRR works when the post-works valuation clears about 133% of all-in cost, since a 75% LTV refinance then returns all the cash.

### Cited Findings

**2a. Cosmetic flip**
- Hamptons 2025: about £16,390 average profit (gross/post-SDLT conflict above). Only **58.7%** of flips profitable after SDLT, and **1.5%** of sales were flips. — [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/); [The Intermediary](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/04/share-of-flipped-homes-falls-to-decade-low-hamptons-finds/)
- Finance: light-refurb bridging from **0.60%/month**, typically 0.60–1.20%. — [Aura Capital](https://www.auracapital.co.uk/bridging-products/light-refurbishment-bridging-loans)
- Rules of thumb for targets: 20% ROI, 20–25% gross margin, 70% rule; expect 10–30% overruns (see section 1). — [Property Geek](https://propertygeek.net/article/how-to-flip/); [Property Accelerator](https://property-accelerator.co.uk/flipping-houses-uk/); [PropAI Deals](https://www.propaideals.co.uk/flip-calculator)

**2b. Heavy refurb flip**
- Heavy refurb bridging costs **0.20–0.40%/month more** than light refurb; first-time developers pay about **1.0–1.5%/month**. Works are funded 100% in arrears in tranches. — [FD Commercial](https://www.fdcommercial.co.uk/finance-guide/property-refurbishment-finance/); [SME Bridging Finance](https://www.smebridgingfinance.co.uk/types/refurbishment-bridging-loans/)
- Contractor risk: FMB State of Trade H2 2025 reports that **nearly half of firms** have jobs delayed by a lack of skilled tradespeople, and H1 2025 found **61%** affected by skills shortages. — [FMB State of Trade H2 2025](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-survey-h2-2025.html); [FMB State of Trade H1 2025](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/state-of-trade-survey-h1-2025.html)

**2c. Extension flip (adding bedroom or floor area)**
- Nationwide research (Oct 2025): a loft conversion or extension adding a double bedroom **and** bathroom can add up to **24%**. Going from 2 to 3 bedrooms adds about **13%**, from 3 to 4 about **10%**, and detached homes up to **17%** for 2→3 beds. A **10% increase in floor space adds about 5%** to value. — [The Intermediary – Nationwide, Oct 2025](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2025/10/loft-conversions-boost-home-value-by-up-to-24-nationwide-research-shows/); [Mortgage Introducer](https://www.mpamag.com/uk/mortgage-types/residential/nationwide-home-improvements-add-up-to-24-to-property-values/553044)
- Build costs: South East mid-range single-storey **£2,200–£3,000/m²** (section 1). — [BestBuilders](https://www.bestbuilders.co.uk/costs/house-extension-cost-uk); [HouseUp](https://www.houseup.co.uk/news/how-much-does-house-extension-cost)

**2d. Conversion: house to flats**
- Conversion cost is quoted at **£1,500–£2,000/m²** where the building is sound and the existing kitchen and bathroom can be reused. — [Extension Architecture – converting a house into two flats](https://extensionarchitecture.co.uk/uncategorized/converting-a-house-into-flats-costs-and-planning-guide/) (merged summary; could be another result in the same set)
- Rental example: a 5-bed Victorian house worth **£400k** rents for about **£2,000/month** as one unit. Split into four self-contained flats at **£750–900** each, it produces **£3,000–3,600/month**. — [HMO Architects – convert a house into flats](https://hmo-architects.com/guides/design/how-to-convert-a-house-into-flats/) (merged summary)
- Planning permission is required in most cases. If you convert your own main residence, capital gains tax can apply. — [Clifton Private Finance](https://www.cliftonpf.co.uk/blog/25042025144422-converting-a-house-into-flats/); [Fox Davidson – converting house to flats finance 2026](https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/2026/04/21/converting-house-to-flats-finance/)
- Finance: heavy-refurb bridge or development finance, at the development rates and LTGDV caps in section 1. — [Fox Davidson](https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/finance-guide/development-finance-rates/)

**2e. HMO conversion**
- Cost per room: **£8,000–£15,000** for a mid-spec Greater Manchester conversion. Totals are **£40,000–£120,000** depending on room count, condition and en-suites. Per-room items include FD30 fire doors (**£800**), emergency lighting (**£250**), smoke and heat detection (**£350**), and insulation and partition (**£1,200**). An extra bathroom (needed above 1 per 3 tenants) is about **£6,500**. — [renovat.co.uk – HMO conversion cost Manchester 2026](https://renovat.co.uk/hmo-conversion-cost-manchester-2026/); [HMO Checker – conversion costs 2026](https://hmochecker.co.uk/hmo-conversion-costs/)
- Article 4 does not ban HMOs; it means planning permission is needed first, which adds **8–13 weeks** and about **£578**. — [HMO Checker](https://hmochecker.co.uk/hmo-conversion-costs/) (merged summary)
- Yield claim: Manchester HMOs average **12.2%** against **4.4%** for standard buy-to-let. This comes from a builder/marketing source, not independent data. — [renovat.co.uk](https://renovat.co.uk/hmo-conversion-cost-manchester-2026/)

**2f. BRRR (buy-refurbish-refinance-rent)**
- Worked example (Apr 2026): ARV of **£150,000**. After **6 months** of ownership, a buy-to-let refinance at **75% LTV** lends **£112,500**, leaving about **£23,100** of cash in the deal. Rent is **£825/month**. — [Estately.uk – BRRRR for auction properties 2026](https://estately.uk/guide/brrrr-strategy-auction-properties/)
- Worked example (Dec 2025): the refinance at 75% LTV returns **£116,250** against **£113,000** invested, so **£0 is left in** and cashflow is **£410/month**. — [PropQuest Solutions – BRR 2025–2026](https://propquestsolutions.co.uk/2025/12/08/brr-strategy-in-2025-2026-how-uk-investors-are-releasing-their-capital-and-recycling-deals-faster-than-ever/)
- Rule of thumb: most good BRRR deals leave some cash in. **£15,000 left in a deal producing £3,000/year is a 20% return** on the cash still working. — [Estately.uk](https://estately.uk/guide/brrrr-strategy-auction-properties/) (merged summary)
- Refinance terms: a clean SPV can borrow up to **75% LTV**, and a few lenders go to 80% at a higher rate. — [Knox Mortgages – BTL rates 2026](https://knoxmortgages.co.uk/buy-to-let-mortgage-rates/); [Property Tax Partners – BTL mortgages 2026](https://www.propertytaxpartners.co.uk/blog/property-finance/buy-to-let-mortgages-guide)

**2g. Title split / garden-plot planning gain** (see section 3 for detail)
- A title-split block-of-flats case study: about **£41,000** of renovation, **£35,179** left in the deal, and about **£260,000** uplift from one building. — [Your Property Network – title-splitting a block of flats, May 2024](https://reader.yourpropertynetwork.co.uk/2024/05/22/title-splitting-a-block-of-flats/content.html)

**2h. Replacement dwelling (knock down and rebuild)**
- A replacement dwelling always needs **full planning permission**, even on the same footprint, but is "usually acceptable in principle". In the **green belt, the new home cannot be materially larger** than the one it replaces. — [Build It – replacement homes planning](https://www.self-build.co.uk/replacement-homes-planning-permission-for-knock-down-and-rebuild-projects/); [Elizabeth Homes – knock down and rebuild guide](https://elizabethhomes.co.uk/guides/knock-down-and-rebuild/)
- A new build can qualify for **VAT relief that a renovation cannot**, which can change which option is best. Replacement dwellings "often perform significantly better financially in high-value locations". — [Elizabeth Homes](https://elizabethhomes.co.uk/insights/knock-down-rebuild-or-renovate/); [Hawkstone Developments – knock down or renovate 2026](https://hawkstone-developments.co.uk/news/should-i-knock-down-my-house-or-renovate-a-complete-uk-guide-for-2026)
- Investors should judge whether they are buying **land value or house value**: when a property is priced around its land, the existing house adds little to the price. — [The Shoreline Agency (Australian source; concept only)](https://www.theshorelineagency.com.au/buyer-glossary/knockdown-rebuild-potential)

**2i. Small new-build scheme (1–10 units)**
- Target **20% on GDV** (15% if pre-sold). Development finance at **0.65–0.90%/month** (established) or **1.10%+** (inexperienced), with first-timer caps of about 85% LTC / 65% LTGDV. — [Construction Capital](https://constructioncapital.co.uk/guides/development-appraisal-lenders); [Fox Davidson](https://www.foxdavidson.co.uk/finance-guide/development-finance-rates/)
- FMB House Builders' Survey 2025: planning is the top barrier (**47%**), followed by materials costs (**41%**), lack of available sites (**33%**) and other land-related barriers (**31%**). **Two-thirds** say they cannot pursue some sites because of anticipated **Section 106** costs, and **over a third** say getting planning permission is getting worse. On the positive side, **73%** expect robust buyer demand, **69%** plan to grow their workforce, and **68%** think the Government's planning reforms can help diversify the market. — [FMB Small House Builders' Survey 2025](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/fmb-house-builders-survey-2025.html); [Housing Today](https://www.housingtoday.co.uk/news/planning-the-biggest-obstacle-for-small-builders-fmb-survey-shows/5126607.article)

**2j. Standard buy-to-let**
- Gross yields (sources vary by method): South East average **6.1%** against a UK average of **6.4%**. - Mortgage rates as of **2 Sept 2026**: average BTL fixes are about **4.9–5.7%**, and best-buy rates start below **3.5%** with large percentage fees. Remortgages at 75% LTV are **4.9–5.5%** (2-year fix) and **4.7–5.2%** (5-year fix). — [Knox Mortgages](https://knoxmortgages.co.uk/buy-to-let-mortgage-rates/); [RemortgageSaver](https://remortgagesaver.com/best-buy-to-let-remortgage-rates/)

The stress rate is about **5.5%** or the product rate +2%, whichever is higher, for 2-year and variable products. Companies deduct interest in full, because Section 24 does not apply to them. — [Property Tax Partners](https://www.propertytaxpartners.co.uk/blog/property-finance/buy-to-let-mortgages-guide); [Visionary Finance](https://visionaryfinance.co.uk/news/limited-company-buy-to-let-btl-hmo-mortgages-tax-efficiency-and-lending-criteria-explained/)

### Inferences
- **(calc) BTL affordability in the South East:** at a 5.5% stress rate and 75% LTV, 125% ICR needs gross rent of at least **5.16% of value**, and 145% needs at least **5.98%**. With South East gross yields of about 5.6–6.1%, **limited-company buyers only just pass at 75% LTV, and higher-rate individuals often cannot**. They must put in more equity, which lowers returns. This helps explain the push toward value-add strategies such as HMOs, BRRR and flats.
- **(calc) Extension-flip economics:** on a £450k South East 3-bed, a fourth bedroom adds about 10% (**about £45k**, Nationwide). A 20 m² single-storey extension at £2,200–£3,000/m² costs about **£44–60k** before fees. **The uplift from extending alone can be at or below cost**, so extension flips in the South East rely on buying below value, adding bedroom *and* bathroom (the up-to-24% case), or using cheaper-per-m² loft or garage conversions. This is an inference; Nationwide's figures are averages.
- **(calc) BRRR break-even:** with a 75% LTV refinance, all cash comes out only if post-works value is at least **133%** of all-in cost (purchase + SDLT + works + fees + finance). With the 5% surcharge adding about 5–7% to acquisition cost, the required uplift has risen.
- **(calc) Small-scheme illustration** (assumptions flagged). Four 110 m² houses (440 m²) at **£2,500/m²** cost £1.10m; fees 10% (£110k); contingency 10% (£110k). With an *assumed* GDV of £2.2m (4 × £550k, not sourced) and 20% profit (£440k), finance of about £95k (0.8%/month on about 50% average drawn over 18 months, before arrangement fees) and sales costs of about 2% (£44k), the **residual land value is roughly £300k** before SDLT on land and CIL/S106. Small changes in GDV or build cost swing land value sharply; that sensitivity is the core risk.
- Timelines, **(inference, low-evidence)**: cosmetic flip about 4–8 months; heavy refurb about 6–12; extension flip about 9–15 including planning or permitted-development checks; house-to-flats or HMO in an Article 4 area 9–18 including planning; BRRR refinance at 6 months minimum (per the Estately example); plot or new-build 18–36 months including planning. These are not directly sourced apart from the 6-month BRRR example, the 8–13 week HMO planning add-on, and the 8-week statutory planning target (section 3).

## 3. Garden-plot / title-split economics and planning risk

### Takeaway
Planning consent typically lifts land value by **about 2.5x to 6x**, with the higher end in the South East and London. Plot value is set by the residual method (GDV − costs − about 20% profit). But planning is slow and uncertain: only **about 38–42%** of minor applications are decided inside the statutory 8 weeks (87–88% when agreed extensions are counted). CIL applies to every new market dwelling unless it is a qualifying self-build. Access and neighbour impact are the usual reasons backland schemes are refused.

### Cited Findings
- Planning permission typically increases land value by **2.5x to 6x**, with higher multipliers in the South East and London. Backland and garden plots in built-up, high-demand areas are named as a key type of uplift opportunity. — [LivedIn – land value planning calculator 2025](https://livedin.co.uk/land-value-planning); [Approved Planning – valuing land with planning](https://www.approved-planning.co.uk/knowledge-base/how-to-value-land-with-planning-permission/); [EBHM Projects – planning uplift](https://ebhmprojects.co.uk/planning-uplift) (merged summary)
- Post-planning plot value is the GDV of the homes, minus construction, professional and finance costs, minus the developer's margin; the uplift is that residual minus the pre-planning value. — [LivedIn](https://livedin.co.uk/land-value-planning) (merged summary)
- Green-belt uplift can be extreme: one London architect headline cites land becoming "**275 times more valuable**". This is an outlier and should not be treated as typical of garden plots. — [Urbanist Architecture – green belt uplift](https://urbanistarchitecture.co.uk/green-belt-land-planning-gain-uplift/)
- Plot prices: outside the most expensive regions, a plot in a good location is typically **£150,000–£250,000**. South East plots are among the most expensive in the UK, and some sell for millions. — [Homebuilding & Renovating – what building plots cost](https://www.homebuilding.co.uk/advice/what-building-plots-cost) (merged summary; no specific South East average found)
- Planning timelines (England, official statistics). Quarter to June 2025: **88%** of minor applications decided within 8 weeks *or agreed time*, but only **42%** within the statutory 8 weeks. Quarter to March 2026: 87% and **38%**. Quarter to June 2026: 87% and **39%**. Councils granted **87%** of all decisions in the year to December 2025 (all application types, not just new dwellings). — [GOV.UK – planning applications Apr–Jun 2025](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/planning-applications-in-england-april-to-june-2025/planning-applications-in-england-april-to-june-2025-statistical-release); [GOV.UK – Jan–Mar 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/planning-applications-in-england-january-to-march-2026/planning-applications-in-england-january-to-march-2026-statistical-release); [GOV.UK – Apr–Jun 2026](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/planning-applications-in-england-april-to-june-2026/planning-applications-in-england-april-to-june-2026-statistical-release)
- CIL is charged per m² at rates each charging authority sets and publishes. The 100 m² minimum threshold **does not apply to new houses or flats, even single units**, so any new market dwelling is potentially liable. **Self-build** homes occupied by the builder or commissioner as their only or main residence are **exempt**. — [Huntingdonshire DC – CIL exemptions and relief](https://www.huntingdonshire.gov.uk/planning/community-infrastructure-levy-cil/cil-process-and-forms/cil-exemptions-and-relief); [Moore Barlow – CIL self-build exemption](https://www.moorebarlow.com/blog/community-infrastructure-levy-and-the-self-build-exemption-everything-you-need-to-know/); [Trueman Brown – CIL guide 2025/26](https://truemanbrown.co.uk/community-infrastructure-levy-guide-2025-26/)
- Backland/garden development: access and neighbours are the main challenges. Common refusal reasons are character and appearance, overlooking, and design. The 2024 NPPF has made councils more receptive, but permission still depends on high-quality design and minimal neighbour impact. Refused schemes are sometimes won on appeal, for example permission in principle for 1–2 dwellings at West Mersea, Colchester, allowed by an inspector. — [Planning House – back garden development](https://planninghouse.co.uk/back-garden-development-unlocking-the-hidden-value-in-residential-plots/); [Facit Homes – backlands planning](https://www.facit-homes.com/post/building-in-your-garden-what-is-backlands-planning-permission); [ADP – appeal success for backland in Colchester](https://www.adpltd.co.uk/latest/planning-appeal-success-for-a-backland-development-in-colchester)
- Selling off part of a garden: owners must confirm on the title deeds that they own the land and where its boundaries are. The value difference with and without planning shapes the sale strategy (sell with consent, or sell subject to planning). — [Property Master Academy – title splitting](https://propertymasteracademy.co.uk/add-value-and-increase-profits-by-title-splitting/); [Bowling & Co – selling part of your garden](https://www.bowlinglaw.co.uk/selling-part-of-your-garden-property/)
- In investor jargon, "title splitting" also means turning one freehold (for example a block of flats) into separate leasehold titles, so each unit can be valued and refinanced or sold separately. — [Property Investors Network – title splitting explained](https://propertyinvestorsnetwork.co.uk/title-splitting-property-explained-how-to-increase-commercial-value/); [HMO Architects – title split strategy](https://hmo-architects.com/guides/investing/title-split-strategy/)
- The FMB 2025 survey found that **two-thirds** of small builders cannot pursue some sites because of anticipated S106 costs, and over a third say planning is getting worse. — [FMB House Builders' Survey 2025](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/fmb-house-builders-survey-2025.html)

### Inferences
- Because only about 4 in 10 minor applications meet the statutory 8 weeks, a garden-plot investor should plan for **3–6+ months** from submission to decision, plus pre-application advice, surveys and design beforehand. Appeals add many months. This is an inference from the cited statistics.
- For a garden-plot or title-split investor, the economics come down to: (post-consent residual plot value) − (pre-consent value) − (planning costs, i.e. architect, surveys, fees, CIL exposure) − (probability-weighted cost of refusal). The 2.5–6x uplift is attractive, but the discount for refusal risk (access, overlooking, character) sets how much can be paid for an option or plot.
- CIL is the easiest cost to overlook in garden-plot appraisals, because the self-build exemption only applies if the plot goes to an owner-occupier self-builder. Selling to a developer or building to sell usually triggers CIL.

### Gaps
- There is **no sourced figure for a typical South East garden-plot value** (e.g. per plot in Surrey, Herts or Kent) or for approval rates on backland/infill applications specifically. No statistics were found on backland appeal success rates.
- CIL £/m² rates for specific South East/East councils were not retrieved.
- Pre-application advice fees and typical architect or planning-consultant fees for a single-plot application were not sourced.

## 4. Deal-finding channels and which segments rely on each

### Takeaway
Rightmove dominates visible supply (over 80% of portal time), so portal deals are the most contested. Experienced flippers and BRRR investors rely on **independent estate agents' "first call" lists**, which take **6–18 months** of reliable buying to break into. **Auctions** are liquid but competitive: well-priced lots in 2026 sold at about 9–23% above reserve or guide. Less experienced or remote investors buy from **deal sourcers** for about **2% or £2–6k** per deal. Developers and plot hunters use **land-sourcing software (about £195+/month)** and **direct-to-vendor letters**, which get **about 1–5% response** (higher when tightly targeted).

### Cited Findings
- **Portals:** Rightmove is the largest UK portal, with over **80%** of time spent on UK property portals and over 80% market share at end-2023. — [Substack analysis of Rightmove](https://technologicallyclairvoyant.substack.com/p/rightmove) (secondary source; treat as indicative)
- **Estate agent relationships:** smaller independent agents are the best route to off-market deals. Build deep relationships with a handful of agents and become their "first call", but "breaking in takes **6–18 months** of consistent presence". — [Galim AI – off-market property UK 2026](https://www.galimai.com/blog/how-to-find-off-market-property-uk.html); [Forth Action Invest](https://forthactioninvest.co.uk/how-to-find-off-market-property-deals-in-the-uk/) (merged summary)
- Agents call first the investors who have **transacted before and complete when they say they will**; "reliability is the currency". Investors should view and offer fast ("most investors simply do not make enough offers"). A reputation for **renegotiating after surveys or withdrawing late** stops off-market introductions. Registering directly with senior negotiators and bringing a one-page buyer profile with proof of funds is recommended. — [Your Property Network – how to find flip deals](https://yourpropertynetwork.co.uk/how-to-find-flip-deals/); [Flambard Williams – off-market deals](https://www.flambardwilliams.co.uk/off-market-property-deals-what-they-are-and-how-to-access-them/); [Galim AI](https://www.galimai.com/blog/how-to-find-off-market-property-uk.html) (merged summary)

Lots sold under the hammer averaged **23.5% above guide** and **15.2% above reserve**. First for Auctions reported an average of **22.9% above reserve** in Q2 2026. - **Deal sourcers:** they sell packaged deals, mainly to BTL, HMO and BRRR investors, often remote ones (see section 6 for fees). Property Tribes members discuss finding reputable sourcers for **remote purchasing**, and payment models such as "**1% of the price or 20% of the saving**". — [Property Tribes – finding a reputable deal sourcer](https://www.propertytribes.com/finding-a-reputable-deal-sourcer-t-127633668.html); [Property Tribes – advice on working with a deal sourcer](https://propertytribes.com/advice-on-working-with-a-deal-sourcer-t-127643516.html)

### Inferences (segment-to-channel mapping; directional, not survey-based)
- **Cosmetic and heavy-refurb flippers:** mainly portal saved searches (reduced, long-listed, "cash buyers only"), independent-agent first-call lists and auctions. Speed and reliable completion are the edge. This follows from the cited agent-relationship guidance and auction data.
- **Extension flippers:** portals and agents, filtering for plot size and extension potential. Being able to judge permitted-development and planning feasibility quickly is the edge.
- **BRRR investors:** auctions, agents, and sourcers, especially when buying remotely in higher-yield areas outside the South East.
- **HMO and house-to-flats converters:** agents, auctions, sourcers, and targeted DTV. Knowing Article 4 boundaries and licensing is essential screening.
- **Garden-plot, title-split and replacement-dwelling investors, and small developers:** land-sourcing software (LandInsight, Searchland, Nimbus), DTV letters to owners of large-garden or corner plots, land agents, and Plotfinder-type listings. FMB's finding that 33% of small builders lack available sites suggests off-market origination matters most in this segment.
- **Standard BTL:** portals, agents, new-build developers' investor sales, and sourcers for remote buyers.
- Across segments, the evidence suggests the **best deals come off-market** (agent first call, DTV, pre-auction), while portals give volume but face the most competition.

### Gaps

## 5. Pain points by investor type

### Takeaway

### Inferences
- **Time spent screening listings** is an implied pain point. Most portal listings fail the 20–25% margin and 70% rules once SDLT and finance are included, and deals must be acted on quickly (agents favour investors who view and offer fast). Screening therefore becomes a high-volume, time-sensitive filtering task. This is inferred from the cited agent guidance and margin maths; no survey quantifies hours spent.
- **Unreliable agent information** (wrong floor areas, tenure, planning history, lease length) is widely reported anecdotally but **was not evidenced here**.
- **Valuation risk:** BRRR returns depend on the refinance valuation reaching about 133% of all-in cost, and flip profit depends on the resale valuation, so a down-valuation removes the recycled capital. Forum evidence could not be retrieved.
- Pain points differ by segment:
  - Flippers: stamp duty, competition, speed.
  - BRRR: valuation, refinance rates and ICR.
  - HMO: Article 4, licensing, fire-safety costs.
  - Plot/new-build: planning time and uncertainty, S106/CIL, site availability, builder costs.
  - BTL: yield versus rates, plus regulatory change (not researched here).

### Gaps
- **First-hand forum, Reddit and YouTube testimony could not be retrieved** (Property Tribes and Property Hub fetches blocked, no Reddit results, search budget exhausted). The thread titles above are the only first-hand evidence captured. A follow-up pass should pull direct quotes.
- No data on how much time investors spend screening per deal, or the ratio of viewings or offers to purchases.
- No sourced evidence on down-valuation frequency for BRRR refinances or flip resales.

## 6. Evidence of willingness to pay for deal-finding (sourcing fees, software subscriptions)

### Takeaway
Investors demonstrably pay for deal flow. **Deal sourcing fees are typically 1–2% of price, often with a £2–6k floor (sometimes "2% or £5,000, whichever is higher")**, and range up to 5%. Software spend runs from **about £14/month** (PropertyData) through **£100–£250/month** (Property Filter) to **about £195+/licence/month** for land-sourcing platforms (Searchland; LandInsight is priced higher). Fees paid per deal (£3–6k) are far larger than typical software subscriptions (£170–£3,000/year).

### Cited Findings
- UK deal sourcing fees typically range from **1% to 5%** of the purchase price. Sourcers commonly charge **£2,000–£6,000 fixed**, or about **1–2%** of price. — [NAPSA – investor guide to sourcer fees](https://napsa.org.uk/hub/investor-guide-to-sourcer-fees); [Property Beacon – sourcing fees guide](https://www.propertybeacon.co.uk/property-sourcing-fees-guide/); [PropSourcer – sourcing fees guide](https://www.propsourcer.com/sourcing-growth-column/property-sourcing-fees-the-comprehensive-guide); [MaddisonV – sourcing fees 2026](https://maddisonvproperties.co.uk/property-sourcing-fees-in-2026-a-comprehensive-guide-for-strategic-investors/) (merged summary)

### Inferences
- **(calc)** A single sourced deal at 2% on a £250k South East property costs **£5,000**. That is about **2 years of Searchland Standard** (£195 × 12 = £2,340/year) or **1.7–4+ years of Property Filter** (£1,200–£3,000/year). Investors pay far more per deal than per year of software, so buyers value *qualified, actionable deals* much more than raw data.
- Willingness to pay appears segmented:
  - Small developers and land traders pay for land platforms (£195+/month) and letter campaigns.
  - BTL, HMO and BRRR investors, especially remote or time-poor ones, pay per-deal sourcing fees.
  - Hands-on flippers use cheap analytics (£14/month) plus their own agent relationships.
- The prevalence of Property Tribes threads about overstated "BMV" deals suggests a trust gap. There is likely demand for independently verifiable deal data (comparables, planning history, true discount) rather than sourcer-asserted figures. This is an inference; no survey supports it.

### Gaps
- No survey data on the **share of investors who use paid sourcers or subscriptions**, or on churn and satisfaction.
- UK LandInsight and Nimbus Maps prices were not confirmed.
- No evidence on fees for land/plot sourcing specifically (e.g. finder's fees as a percentage of land value, or option-agreement promotion fees).
