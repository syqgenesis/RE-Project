# UK Residential Property Investment Value Chain: Who's Who (excluding deal-finding software)

Research note, compiled 28 September 2026. Scope: the organisations a flipper, landlord or small developer deals with at each stage (find, finance, buy, add value, sell/let). Audience: non-technical founder new to real estate.

**Method caveat (read first):** Direct page fetches were blocked for almost every domain in this environment (Rightmove plc, EIG, Wikipedia, trade press, lender sites, gov.uk). All findings below come from web-search result snippets and summaries, not full-page reads. Figures are cited to the page the snippet came from, but I could not open the page to check context. Where a snippet looked garbled, secondary or self-promotional, I have flagged it. The search budget ran out before I could cover every named player. Players I could not verify in this session are listed under **Gaps** with a "[background knowledge, unverified]" tag so the report writer can decide whether to include them.

**Value chain map (for orientation):**
- **Find:** portals (Rightmove, Zoopla, OnTheMarket, plot portals), estate agents (and their investor lists), auction houses and aggregators (EIG), deal sourcers.
- **Finance:** brokers and packagers (the gatekeepers), bridging lenders, buy-to-let (BTL) lenders, development lenders, crowdfunding and P2P platforms, and government schemes (Homes England, British Business Bank).
- **Buy:** conveyancers and solicitors, surveyors and valuers (RICS), and auctioneers' legal packs.
- **Add value:** architects (RIBA), planning consultants (RTPI), quantity surveyors (RICS), builders (FMB).
- **Sell/let:** agents again, portals again, letting agents, and the redress schemes, regulators and trade bodies that govern them.
- **Around all of it:** education and community businesses (Property Hub, Progressive, pin, gurus) and trade media.

## 1. Portals: Rightmove, Zoopla, OnTheMarket and niche portals

### Takeaway
Rightmove is by far the biggest UK property portal. In 2025 it had about 19,300 paying advertisers and, by its own figures, about 89% of the time people spent on UK property portals. Zoopla (owned by Houseful, which is backed by Silver Lake) is second. OnTheMarket (owned by CoStar since December 2023) is a distant but well-funded third. None of the three offers investors an open data feed: Rightmove's terms ban automated access, and its data feed is only for member agents. Niche portals such as PlotBrowser and Plotfinder list land and self-build plots.

### Cited Findings
**Rightmove (Rightmove plc, FTSE 100)**
- FY2025 revenue was £425.1m, up 9% — [PropertyWire](https://www.propertywire.com/news/rightmove-reports-9-revenue-growth-to-425-1m-in-2025/); also [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/marketing-news/rightmove-revenue-up-9-as-agent-spending-rises/)
- At end-2025, total advertisers rose 1% to 19,272. Agency branches rose 2% to 16,385. New homes developments numbered 2,887 (down 36 year on year) — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/rightmove-revenue-up-9-as-growth-shifts-away-from-core-agency-price-rises/); primary: [Rightmove FY25 presentation](https://plc.rightmove.co.uk/content/uploads/2026/02/260227-FY25-Presentation.pdf) (could not open)
- ARPA (average revenue per advertiser, per month) rose 6% to £1,621. Agency ARPA was £1,530 (+6%) and new homes ARPA was £2,135 (+7%) — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/rightmove-revenue-up-9-as-growth-shifts-away-from-core-agency-price-rises/)
- 35% of independent agents subscribe to Optimiser Edge, Rightmove's top-tier agency package — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/rightmove-revenue-up-9-as-growth-shifts-away-from-core-agency-price-rises/)
- Rightmove had 89% of all time spent on UK property portals in 2025, up from 83% in 2024, according to its FY2025 results (a company-reported metric) — [Pine guide](https://getpine.co.uk/guides/rightmove-vs-zoopla-for-sellers) / [Propelr guide](https://www.propelr.co.uk/guides/rightmove-vs-zoopla-for-sellers)
- Rightmove is said to account for "over 70% of portal-driven instructions", and "virtually every estate agent in England and Wales lists on Rightmove". These are secondary claims from a comparison guide and are unverified against primary data — [Pine guide](https://getpine.co.uk/guides/rightmove-vs-zoopla-for-sellers)
- Rightmove plc's 2025 results coverage headlined an "AI push" — [Property Soup](https://propertysoup.co.uk/rightmove-plc-posts-9-revenue-rise-as-ai-push-gathers-pace/)

**Zoopla (Houseful, formerly ZPG; backed by Silver Lake)**
- Zoopla is owned by Silver Lake-backed Houseful and is the UK's number-two portal — [Pine guide](https://getpine.co.uk/guides/rightmove-vs-zoopla-for-sellers)
- Zoopla's Q1 revenues rose 9%, and its "homeowner audience" topped six million — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/zoopla-q1-revenues-rise-9-as-homeowner-audience-tops-six-million/)
- Dexters signed a deal to use Zoopla's Valuation Booster across all its branches until December 2026. Zoopla also launched hyperlocal, ROI-based packages for agents — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/as-onthemarket-hits-15000-advertisers-zoopla-fights-back-with-big-agency-deal/)
- Trade press speculated about CoStar buying Zoopla (Sept 2025). This was an opinion piece, not a deal — [Estate Agent Today](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/features/2025/09/property-natter-agents-imagine-a-world-where-costar-buys-zoopla/)

**OnTheMarket (CoStar Group)**
- CoStar bought OnTheMarket for about £99m. The deal was announced in October 2023 and completed on 12 December 2023. OnTheMarket was launched in 2015 by agents trying to reduce their dependence on Rightmove — [Wikipedia: OnTheMarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OnTheMarket) (snippet only); [Pine guide](https://getpine.co.uk/guides/rightmove-vs-zoopla-for-sellers)
- CoStar committed roughly £45–47m a year to UK consumer marketing. OnTheMarket reached 15,000 agent advertisers — [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/as-onthemarket-hits-15000-advertisers-zoopla-fights-back-with-big-agency-deal/); [Online Marketplaces](https://www.onlinemarketplaces.com/articles/onthemarket-says-costar-backing-delivering-traffic-stock-and-leads-surge/)
- One commentator's summary of agent sentiment: "Rightmove remains mandatory, Zoopla remains the default number two, and OnTheMarket is a monitored third option" — [Dwellio / Coraly comparison pages](https://dwellio.co.uk/blog/alternatives-to-rightmove) (secondary)

**Scraping and data-feed rules**
- Rightmove's terms say: "You must not use or attempt to use any automated program (including, without limitation, any spider or other web crawler) to access our system or this Site" — quoted in [Actowiz](https://www.actowizsolutions.com/scrape-rightmove-uk-property-data-2026.php) (a scraping vendor, so secondary)
- Rightmove's datafeed requires agent membership and is not available to investors or analytics platforms — [Actowiz](https://www.actowizsolutions.com/scrape-rightmove-uk-property-data-2026.php) (secondary)
- Many third-party scrapers for Rightmove, Zoopla and OnTheMarket are sold on Apify, which shows demand despite the terms — [Apify Rightmove scraper](https://apify.com/automation-lab/rightmove-scraper); [Apify multi-portal scraper](https://apify.com/jungle_synthesizer/rightmove-zoopla-onthemarket-uk-scraper/api/python)

**Niche portals**
- **PlotBrowser:** a free UK listing of building plots and renovation or conversion opportunities, run by *Build It* magazine. Plots must have existing planning permission to be listed. Buyers must register to see seller details — [PlotBrowser: About](https://www.plotbrowser.com/guides/aboutplotbrowser); [PlotBrowser listing guide](https://www.plotbrowser.com/guides/listing)
- **Plotfinder:** a subscription land and renovation finder. It charges about £5 a month or £40–£60 a year to see full listings — [Plotfinder](https://www.plotfinder.net/); fee detail via [Fotheringham Homes review](https://fotheringhamhomes.com/2018/08/27/the-3-best-self-build-plot-finder-websites-reviewed/) (2018, so the fee may be out of date)
- **BuildStore PlotSearch:** another self-build plot database — [BuildStore PlotSearch](https://plotsearch.buildstore.co.uk/findingland/)
- **EIG (Essential Information Group):** the main aggregator of UK property auction lots and results (see Section 3) — [EIG auction results](https://www.eigpropertyauctions.co.uk/search/auction-results)

### Inferences
- For a startup, the portal oligopoly is the main constraint on data: all three portals are agent-funded businesses that protect their listings data. Anyone building investor tools on portal data faces terms-of-service and legal risk. The report writer should cross-refer to the deal-finding-software researcher on this.
- Rightmove's pricing power over agents (ARPA rising about 6% a year while advertiser numbers are flat) is a recurring grievance. That grievance is why OnTheMarket was founded and why CoStar is spending heavily.

### Gaps
- I could not open Rightmove's FY25 report, so I could not confirm its data products (e.g. Rightmove Data Services, Rightmove Plus, mortgages, commercial) or 2026 guidance.
- I found no reliable current figures for Zoopla's or OnTheMarket's audience share, or for Zoopla's agent count.
- Zoopla's and OnTheMarket's exact terms on scraping and feeds were not checked. [Background knowledge, unverified] Both ban automated scraping in their terms, and both take listings from agents via feeds and CRM integrations (e.g. Zoopla's ZPG feed, OnTheMarket feeds via Reapit, Alto and similar).
- I did not verify any H1 2026 Rightmove results.

## 2. Estate agents: national chains and how they deal with investors

### Takeaway
UK estate agency is fragmented, but a few big groups stand out. Connells Group (owned by Skipton Building Society, and owner of Countrywide) has 1,200+ branches and about a 10% share of estate agency. Private-equity roll-ups such as LRG and Lomond are growing through acquisitions. Foxtons dominates in London. Savills, Knight Frank and Strutt & Parker cover the prime market. Purplebricks, now owned by Strike, is the main online/hybrid brand but has been financially fragile. Agents feed investors through informal "investor lists" and off-market deals. Deal sourcers who act as intermediaries must register with HMRC for anti-money-laundering (AML) supervision, join a redress scheme and register with the ICO.

### Cited Findings
- **Connells Group:** the UK's largest high-street estate agency and property group, with about 10% market share in estate agency, 80+ brands and 1,200+ branches. It is a subsidiary of Skipton Building Society — [Connells Group](https://www.connellsgroup.co.uk/); [Connells brands](https://www.connellsgroup.co.uk/our-group/our-brands/); [Wikipedia: Connells Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connells_Group) (snippet)
- Connells completed its purchase of Countrywide plc on 8 March 2021, creating a network of 1,235 branches — [Wikipedia: Connells Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connells_Group) (snippet)
- Connells made a £28.4m profit in H1 2025 (H1 2024: £20.0m), helped by the stamp duty deadline. It swung to a £0.5m loss in H1 2026 — [PropertyWire](https://www.propertywire.com/news/connells-group-reports-500000-loss-in-first-half-of-2026/); [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/connells-moves-from-profit-to-loss-skiptons-results-reveal/)
- **LRG (formerly Leaders Romans Group):** bought the 50+ branch independent Chancellors Group, its biggest acquisition — [LonRes Movers & Shakers Apr 2025](https://lonres.com/public/property-movers-shakers-apr-2025/) (snippet; the deal date was not confirmed)
- **Lomond Group:** 14 estate and letting agencies, now owned by ICG. It grew from about 350 to about 3,000 staff in four years through acquisitions. Its London flagship, Kinleigh Folkard & Hayward, has 60+ branches. It made its sixth London acquisition of 2025 in October 2025 — [LonRes](https://lonres.com/public/property-movers-shakers-apr-2025/); [Estate Agent Today](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/10/lomond-secures-sixth-london-acquisition-of-2025/)
- **Foxtons (LSE-listed, London):** 2025 revenue £172.5m. Lettings revenue rose 5% to £111m, so lettings are about two-thirds of revenue. Profit was £22.2m, flat — [Letting Agent Today](https://www.lettingagenttoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2026/03/lettings-revenue-up-but-profit-flat-for-muted-foxtons/)
- **Purplebricks (owned by Strike):** Strike bought Purplebricks in 2023, and Strike estate agents now trade under the Purplebricks brand — [Purplebricks: Strike is now Purplebricks](https://www.purplebricks.co.uk/services/strike-is-now-purplebricks); [Freston Ventures](https://frestonventures.com/news/strike-aquires-purple-bricks.php)
- Strike's 2024 accounts flagged a "material uncertainty" over whether it could continue as a going concern, with significant losses expected to September 2025. HMRC filed a winding-up petition, which Purplebricks called an "admin error" and said was being withdrawn. Strike raised £34m — [Dealroom](https://app.dealroom.co/news/note/purplebricks-parent-strike-lands-34m-as-hmrc-files-winding-up-petition); [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/agencies-people-news/shock-purplebricks-at-risk-of-going-bust-owner-strike-reveals/)
- **Savills / Knight Frank:** both focus on prime, high-value residential with global networks. This characterisation comes from a secondary list — [startood Top 50 estate agents](https://startood.com/estate-agents-in-the-uk/) (weak source)
- **Hamptons:** has its own Wikipedia entry and is listed among Connells Group brands — [Wikipedia: Hamptons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamptons_(estate_agent)) (snippet only; see Gaps)
- **How agents and intermediaries deal with investors:** anyone doing "estate agency work", including deal sourcing, must register with HMRC for AML supervision before trading, belong to a redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or Property Redress Scheme) and register with the ICO if they hold investor or seller data — [PropSourcer compliance checklist](https://www.propsourcer.com/sourcing-growth-column/the-uk-property-sourcing-compliance-checklist); [Savvy Property Investor](https://www.savvypropertyinvestor.com/property-sourcer-compliance.html); [L&M Property Sourcing](https://www.lmpropertysourcing.co.uk/blog/property-redress-scheme-prs-tpo-2026)
- Trading as an estate agency business without HMRC AML registration is a criminal offence — [GOV.UK: estate and letting agency AML guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/money-laundering-regulations-2007-supervision-of-estate-agency-businesses) (snippet); [Complyport](https://complyport.com/understanding-hmrcs-role-in-aml-supervision/)

### Inferences
- For investors, the relationship with an agent is the main "find" channel outside portals and auctions. Agents keep private lists of cash-ready investors and pass them properties that need work, probate sales and chain-free quick sales before (or instead of) listing them on portals. This is widely described but I could not source it quantitatively in this session.
- Consolidation (Connells/Countrywide; PE-backed LRG and Lomond roll-ups) means a small number of groups control a large share of branches and lettings books. These groups are also the channel partners portals and lenders court.
- Most chains cross-sell mortgages, conveyancing and surveys (e.g. Connells' financial-services arms). That makes agents gatekeepers for ancillary services too.

### Gaps
- LRG's current branch count and owner. [Background knowledge, unverified] LRG is owned by Bridgepoint and has several hundred branches across brands including Leaders, Romans, Chancellors and Hunters.
- Hamptons' ownership. [Background knowledge, unverified] Hamptons was part of Countrywide and so is now within Connells.
- Strutt & Parker's ownership. [Background knowledge, unverified] It has been owned by BNP Paribas since 2018; I could not confirm any 2025–26 change of ownership.
- Savills and Knight Frank UK residential revenue and branch counts were not retrieved.
- There is no quantitative source on the share of investor purchases that come off-market or through agent investor lists.

## 3. Auctions: houses, market size, investor share and the modern method

### Takeaway
UK property auctions set records in 2025 according to EIG: 41,628 lots offered, 28,975 sold and about £5.9bn raised. Residential accounts for most of the volume (about £4.5bn over Dec 2024 to Nov 2025). Allsop calls itself the largest auction house by value. Auction House UK is the largest franchise network by branch count. iamsold dominates the "modern method of auction" (MMoA), which has drawn regulatory and consumer criticism over non-refundable reservation fees. Investors and "opportunistic" buyers dominate auction rooms, and most buy with cash.

### Cited Findings
**Market size (EIG, the market's main data source)**
- 2025 full year: 41,628 lots offered and 28,975 sold, the highest annual totals on record. Funds raised were £5.9bn (+7.1%). Lots offered rose 14.2% and lots sold rose 15.1% year on year — [BeBeez International](https://bebeez.eu/2026/03/23/property-auction-market-hits-new-benchmarks-as-supply-and-sales-volumes-surge/) citing EIG; primary: [EIG Q4 2025 insights](https://www.eigpropertyauctions.co.uk/news/pad/2025/q4) (could not open)
- Residential only, December 2024 to November 2025: 36,441 lots offered (+4.7%) and revenue of £4,468.1m (+7%) — [BeBeez International](https://bebeez.eu/2025/12/19/property-auction-sales-jump-nearly-18-year-on-year/) citing EIG
- EIG publishes quarterly "Property Auction Insights", and a Q1 2026 edition exists — [EIG Q1 2026](https://www.eigpropertyauctions.co.uk/news/pad/2026/q1) (not opened)

**Buyer mix (Savills survey)**
- In a Savills survey of just under 500 respondents, investors were the largest buyer group at 25%, followed by opportunistic buyers (22%) and buyers for their own use (18%). 68% funded purchases with cash. The most common aim was to refurbish or redevelop (33%), then residential investment (25%) — [Savills blog](https://www.savills.co.uk/blog/article/374670/property-auctions/who-s-in-the-auction-market-and-what-are-they-buying-.aspx). **Flag:** the survey date is unclear ("end of last year"). It may be from 2024 or earlier, and I could not open the page.

**Auction houses**
- **Allsop:** describes itself as the UK's largest property auction house. It raised £1bn in 2024. Its December 2025 residential auction was its largest-ever December sale: over £70m, 186 lots sold, 80% success rate. Its November 2025 residential sale raised £54m (160 lots, 78%) — [Allsop insights](https://www.allsop.co.uk/insights/1bn-raised-in-2024-what-does-2025-hold-for-the-auction-market/); [Property Investor Today](https://www.propertyinvestortoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/12/allsop-raises-over-70m-in-largest-ever-december-residential-auction/); [Estates Gazette](https://www.estatesgazette.co.uk/news/allsop-raises-54m-at-november-residential-auction/); [Allsop Jan 2025](https://www.allsop.co.uk/insights/allsop-raises-32-million-at-first-residential-auction-of-2025/)
- **Auction House UK:** a franchise network of about 50 regional branches selling about 6,000 lots a year, running both traditional and modern-method auctions. It is described as the largest residential auction network by branch count — [YourPropertyCheck guide](https://yourpropertycheck.co.uk/guides/best-uk-property-auction-houses) (secondary; unverified against Auction House's own figures)
- **Savills Auctions:** holds regular national/London auctions, with about a 79% success rate quoted in a secondary guide — [Savills Auctions](https://auctions.savills.co.uk/); [YourPropertyCheck](https://yourpropertycheck.co.uk/guides/best-uk-property-auction-houses) (secondary)
- **iamsold:** runs the modern method of auction only. The buyer pays a reservation fee or premium (quoted as 4.2% including VAT) and the seller pays 0% commission — [YourPropertyCheck](https://yourpropertycheck.co.uk/guides/best-uk-property-auction-houses) (secondary); [iamsold MMoA terms](https://www.iamsold.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/iamsold-Modern-Method-Terms-Conditions_A4P-8P-1044-22-10-V1.pdf)
- **Clive Emson (South/South-East/West):** 754 properties sold in the past 12 months, raising about £150.5m. It is ranked 8th of the top 50 UK auction houses by an aggregator — [propertyauctions.news](https://propertyauctions.news/auctioneer/clive-emson/) (aggregator; unverified); [Clive Emson](https://www.cliveemson.co.uk/)
- **Pugh & Company (North West/national):** established 1991, a leading residential auction house holding about 12 auctions a year — search snippet via [propertyauctions.io / aggregator](https://propertyauctions.io/auctioneers/cliveemson) (weak; see Gaps)
- Other large regional auctioneers named in comparisons include Bond Wolfe and Romans Auction — [YourPropertyCheck](https://yourpropertycheck.co.uk/guides/best-uk-property-auction-houses); [Property Auction Action](https://www.propertyauctionaction.co.uk/9-property-auction-houses-vs-romans-auction-expert-uk-market-guide-2025/); Edward Mellor (Manchester) publishes its own round-up — [Edward Mellor 2025 round-up](https://edwardmellor.co.uk/news/2025-auction-round-up/)

**Modern method of auction: criticism**
- Buyers pay a non-refundable reservation fee, usually a percentage of the price. Complaints include buyers believing the fee would be deducted from the price or refunded if the sale fell through. The Ombudsman singled out MMoA because it "can look like a normal agency sale while operating on entirely different fees, deadlines and commitment points" — [Today's Wills and Probate](https://todayswillsandprobate.co.uk/traditional-vs-modern-methods-of-property-auction-a-divided-landscape-2/); [ReadySteadySell](https://www.readysteadysell.co.uk/modern-method-of-auction-the-truth-and-facts/)
- Propertymark's chief executive said a reservation fee "needs to be transparent and while there is no limit, it would need to be 'reasonable'" — [Estate Agent Today (2019)](https://www.estateagenttoday.co.uk/uncategorised/2019/02/warning-over-reservation-fees-in-the-modern-method-of-auction/)
- Some traditional auctioneers have called MMoA "a fee generating scam" — [Today's Wills and Probate](https://todayswillsandprobate.co.uk/traditional-vs-modern-methods-of-property-auction-a-divided-landscape-2/)
- HomeOwners Alliance has investigated online auction fees, including commissions of up to 12.5% — [HOA](https://hoa.org.uk/news/hoa-investigates-would-you-pay-an-agent-12-5-commission-to-sell-your-home/)

### Inferences
- About £6bn a year in sales and about 29,000 lots sold makes auctions a small share of UK housing transactions (roughly 1.1–1.2m a year, a figure not verified this session). But auctions are disproportionately important for flippers: distressed, probate, repossessed and "needs work" stock is concentrated there, and a completion deadline (usually 28 days) drives demand for bridging finance.
- EIG is both the market's data source and a lot aggregator, which makes it a key "find" channel and a likely partner or competitor for any investor-tools startup.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve EIG's league table of top auctioneers by lots or value for 2025.
- iamsold's annual volumes were not found.
- Savills Auctions' 2025 totals were not found.
- Pugh's details come from a weak snippet. [Background knowledge, unverified] Pugh is based in the North West and was historically linked to the Countrywide/Connells auction network.
- Share of auction purchases funded by bridging: not found beyond the "68% cash" (possibly dated) Savills figure.

## 4. Lenders: bridging, buy-to-let, development finance and government schemes

### Takeaway
Specialist, non-bank and challenger lenders dominate investor finance. Bridging is a roughly £13bn loan-book market represented by the BDLA (formerly ASTL). Buy-to-let is a £311.6bn stock market with £40.3bn of 2025 lending, increasingly written by specialist lenders (Paragon, OSB brands, Together, LendInvest). Development finance for small developers comes from challenger banks, specialist lenders, crowdfunding platforms and Homes England. **Major risk flag:** Market Financial Solutions (MFS), once one of the biggest bridging lenders (about £2.4bn loan book), went into administration in February 2026 amid alleged double-pledging fraud with a reported shortfall of about £930m.

### Cited Findings
**Bridging market size and data**
- The Association of Short Term Lenders (ASTL) rebranded as the **Bridging & Development Lenders Association (BDLA)** on 24 June 2024 — [BDLA](https://thebdla.org/news/astl-rebrands-to-bdla/); [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/astl-announces-rebrand-to-become-bdla/)
- Members' bridging loan books passed £8bn (£8.1bn) in Q1 2024 — [BDLA](https://thebdla.org/news/bridging-loan-books-break-through-8bn/); [Mortgage Solutions](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/specialist-lending/2024/05/30/bridging-loan-books-breach-8bn-in-q1-astl/)
- BDLA's collective loan book is "just under £13bn", with close to 100 lender and associate members. Loan books were £10.3bn by Q4 2024 and annual lending passed £13bn by end-2025. **Flag:** these figures came from an AI search summary. The likely source is the [Funding Agent statistics page](https://www.fundingagent.co.uk/post/bridging-finance-statistics-uk), which is secondary; verify with [BDLA](https://thebdla.org/).
- **BDLA Q2 2026:** bridging and development completions were £1.6bn (down 15.2% on the quarter) and applications £7.3bn (down 26.3%) — [The Intermediary, Sept 2026](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/09/bridging-and-development-lending-completions-fall-15-2-in-q2-bdla/)
- **Bridging Trends** (a broker/packager-compiled index): Q2 2026 contributor gross lending was £173.1m, down 15% from Q1's £199.2m. Chain-break prevention and investment-property purchase were each 18% of loans. Regulated bridging rose to 48% (from 41%). Average LTV was 55% and the average monthly rate 0.81%. **Flag:** the completion-time figure was garbled in the snippet (either 46 or 53 days) — [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/bridging-lending-dips-by-15-in-q2-to-173m/); [IFA Magazine](https://ifamagazine.com/borrowers-maximise-bridgings-versatility/)
- Bridging Trends full year: £811m in 2025 against £822.2m in 2024. Investment-property purchase was the most popular use in 2025 (20%) — [MT Finance](https://www.mt-finance.com/bridging-market-outlook-for-2025-trends-and-opportunities-ahead/); [MT Finance](https://www.mt-finance.com/speed-and-stability-bridging-loan-completion-times-hit-eight-year-low-in-2025/)
- Bridging Trends is compiled from specialist finance packagers and brokers including AFIG, Brightstar Financial, Capital B, Clever Lending, Clifton Private Finance, Complete FS, Enness, Impact Specialist Finance, LDNFinance, Optimum Elite, Sirius Finance and UK Property Finance. MT Finance (a lender) runs it — search summary; see [Bridging Trends on X](https://x.com/bridgingtrends) and [MT Finance](https://www.mt-finance.com/)
- Note that the two data series measure different things: BDLA is lender-reported (£1.6bn a quarter in completions), while Bridging Trends covers only the contributing brokers (about £0.2bn a quarter).

- **Together (Together Financial Services):** a specialist lender covering bridging, BTL and commercial loans. Its loan book reached a record £8.5bn in 2026, with annual profit of £223m. ## 5. Brokers and packagers: numbers and their role as gatekeepers

### Takeaway
About 9 in 10 UK mortgages now go through intermediaries (IMLA forecast: 89% in 2025, 91% in 2026). Most specialist lenders (bridging, development, much BTL) lend only through brokers. The NACFB, the main commercial finance broker body, has 1,414 member firms and 3,011 individual brokers. Specialist "packagers" sit between brokers and lenders and aggregate complex cases.

### Cited Findings

## 7. Trade bodies and regulators

### Takeaway
UK estate agency is only lightly regulated. There is no licensing, but agents must join a redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or Property Redress Scheme) and register with HMRC for AML supervision. The lettings side is being reshaped by the Renters' Rights Act 2025, which abolished "no-fault" Section 21 evictions from 1 May 2026 and brings a landlord database and a landlord ombudsman. The main trade bodies are the NRLA (landlords, 100,000+ members), Propertymark (agents, 18,711 members), the BDLA (bridging and development lenders), the NACFB (commercial brokers), RICS, FMB and the HBF.

### Cited Findings
- **NRLA (National Residential Landlords Association):** the UK's largest landlord association, with over 100,000 members. It publishes guidance on the Renters' Rights Act and BTL market updates — [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/); [NRLA Renters' Rights guidance](https://www.nrla.org.uk/resources/renters-rights/existing-tenancies-renters-rights-act)
- **Propertymark** (the umbrella body for NAEA and ARLA): membership ended 2025 at 18,711 — [Propertymark Annual Report 2025](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/static/27a50d49-c359-4d14-b4f6630489a99022/25c52a9c-f4c8-46c5-bad684330a40b9af/propertymark-annual-report.pdf); [Propertymark conduct rules](https://www.propertymark.co.uk/static/1b740fbb-8cc0-4ed4-8a5bd9b5d006d765/75f8bfdf-f1b8-471f-8b342051082cc258/propertymark-conduct-and-membership-rules.pdf)
- **The Property Ombudsman (TPO):** over 30,000 UK property agent offices registered. **Flag:** from a search summary; verify against [TPO register](https://www.tpos.co.uk/register-of-businesses/) / [TPO businesses](https://www.tpos.co.uk/businesses/)

Businesses must register before trading and carry out customer due diligence on both parties. Letting agents are in scope for lettings at or above a monthly rent threshold, quoted in a snippet as "£10,000". **Flag:** the regulations set this threshold in euros (€10,000), so check — [GOV.UK guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/money-laundering-regulations-2007-supervision-of-estate-agency-businesses); [GOV.UK: who needs to register](https://gov.uk/guidance/money-laundering-regulations-who-needs-to-register); [OneID](https://oneid.uk/news-and-events/aml-and-kyc-for-estate-and-letting-agents-hmrc-rules)
- **BDLA (formerly ASTL):** the trade body for bridging and development lenders; see Section 4 — [BDLA about](https://thebdla.org/about/)
- **NACFB:** see Section 5 — [NACFB](https://nacfb.org/)
- **Renters' Rights Act 2025** (MHCLG): Royal Assent on 27 October 2025. Section 21 was abolished from 1 May 2026, so landlords must now prove a statutory Section 8 ground for possession. Registration on the private rented sector database is reported to open on 15 December 2026, with a landlord ombudsman and Decent Homes Standard in later phases — [Wikipedia: Renters' Rights Act 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters'_Rights_Act_2025) (snippet); [legislation.gov.uk Commencement No. 2 Regs 2026](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2026/421/contents/made); [GOV.UK information sheet 2026](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/69bc04b8f7b1c24d8e23ce60/The_Renters__Rights_Act_Information_Sheet_2026.pdf); [Osbornes Law](https://osborneslaw.com/blog/section-21-abolition-key-dates/). **Flag:** the 15 December 2026 database date comes from a secondary source.
- **Homes England:** the government housing agency that runs the Home Building Fund and lending alliances (Section 4) — [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/levelling-up-home-building-fund-development-finance)
- Consumer and regulatory commentary has long called for property training to be regulated (Section 8) — [Change.org petition](https://www.change.org/p/uk-parliament-regulations-of-the-property-investment-training-sector)

### Inferences
- Because redress membership and HMRC AML registration are legal must-haves for anyone doing "estate agency work", a startup that introduces buyers to sellers for a fee (for example, deal sourcing) would itself become a regulated estate agency business.
- The Renters' Rights Act raises compliance costs for small landlords. That is a likely reason small landlords are selling and investors are shifting towards HMOs, serviced accommodation or flipping, though this was not sourced here.

## 8. Education and community: Property Hub, Progressive, pin, Property Tribes, gurus

### Takeaway
UK property investing has a large, unregulated education and training industry. It ranges from respected free content (Property Hub's *The Property Podcast*, 100k+ downloads a month), through networking events (Progressive Property Network; Property Investors Network), to high-ticket coaching (Samuel Leeds charges up to £12,000 a year). The FCA does not regulate property training. Critics, including Tax Policy Associates and landlord media, say parts of the sector sell hard, give wrong tax advice and push students into in-house deal-sourcing, finance and investment products.

### Cited Findings
- **Property Hub (Rob Dix and Rob Bence):** publishes *The Property Podcast*, described as the UK's number-one, most-downloaded and longest-running property podcast, with 100,000+ downloads a month and new episodes every Tuesday and Thursday — [Property Podcast site](https://podcast.propertyhub.net/); [Listen Notes](https://www.listennotes.com/podcasts/the-property-podcast-rob-bence-and-rob-dix-29iguGXL3xD/); [Property Hub: About](https://propertyhub.net/about/) (self-reported)
- **Progressive Property (Rob Moore and Mark Homer, founded 2003):** a property education company running courses and events. Its **Progressive Property Network (PPN)** describes itself as the largest network of monthly property meetings (about 50 UK locations), with events launching in 6 countries — [Progressive: About](https://progressiveproperty.co.uk/about-us/); [Progressive co-founders](https://progressiveproperty.co.uk/our-co-founders/); [PPN Liverpool](https://progressivepropertynetwork.co.uk/property-networking-revolution-liverpool/) (self-reported)
- **Conflict flag on pin (Property Investors Network):** one search summary said Progressive "created the Property Investors Network (PIN)". This conflicts with my background knowledge that pin was founded by Simon Zutshi and is a separate business. I could not verify either way; treat the search summary as unreliable.
- **Samuel Leeds (Property Investors Academy / Samuel Leeds Property Education):** described as a "property guru" who uses hard-sell tactics to sell courses, charges up to £12,000 for 12 months of coaching and claims a £20m portfolio. Tax Policy Associates (January 2026) said many of his tax claims are wrong. - The education and community layer is where many new flippers and landlords form their habits and choose their suppliers. ## 9. Media: trade press and investor publications

### Takeaway
UK property media splits into agent and industry trade press (Property Industry Eye, The Negotiator, Estate Agent Today, Letting Agent Today, Estates Gazette, Property Week), investor and landlord titles (Property Investor Today, Landlord Today, Property Investor News, Property118, LandlordZone, YPN), and finance trade press (Bridging & Commercial, Mortgage Strategy, Mortgage Solutions, The Intermediary). These outlets are where portal, lender and regulatory news breaks, and they are useful PR and advertising channels for a startup.
