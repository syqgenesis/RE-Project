## 1. Stamp duty land tax (SDLT): surcharge, thresholds, Multiple Dwellings Relief, 2025/26 Budgets

### Takeaway
Stamp duty has moved steadily against investors: the second-home/buy-to-let surcharge rose from 3% to 5% on 30 October 2024, bulk-purchase relief (MDR) was abolished from 1 June 2024, and the temporary threshold cuts of 2020–21 and 2022–25 have all expired, leaving the nil-rate band at £125k from 1 April 2025. I found no evidence of further SDLT rate changes in the November 2025 Budget (its big property measures were income tax and a new "mansion" council tax surcharge), but could not confirm this directly.

### Cited Findings
- **8 July 2020 to 30 June 2021 [expired]:** SDLT "holiday" removed stamp duty on the first £500,000 of a residential purchase; from 1 July to 30 September 2021 the nil band was £250,000. — [Reeds Rains](https://www.reedsrains.co.uk/blog/stamp-duty-2021-changes-explained); [Unbiased](https://www.unbiased.co.uk/discover/mortgages-property/buying-a-home/stamp-duty-holiday-end-what-was-it-and-what-effect-did-it-have)
- **1 April 2021 [IN FORCE]:** 2% SDLT surcharge for non-UK residents buying residential property in England and Northern Ireland; it continued to apply throughout later relief periods. — [GoFile Knowledgebase](https://gofile.co.uk/knowledgebase/stamp-duty-land-tax/non-resident-surcharge/); [Deloitte TaxScape](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/article/stamp-duty-land-tax--non-uk-resident-surcharge.aspx)
- **23 September 2022 to 31 March 2025 [expired]:** nil-rate band was £250,000 (raised in the September 2022 mini-budget); first-time buyer relief was also enhanced during this period. — [Simpkins Edwards](https://www.simpkinsedwards.co.uk/articles/sdlt-higher-rate-for-additional-dwellings-increased); [Rayner Essex](https://rayneressex.com/news/stamp-duty-land-tax-rate-changes/)
- **1 June 2024 [IN FORCE]:** Multiple Dwellings Relief (MDR) abolished. MDR had let a buyer of two or more dwellings in one transaction pay SDLT based on the *average* price per dwelling, which greatly cut tax on portfolio and block purchases. Contracts exchanged on or before 6 March 2024 (the Spring Budget announcement date) kept the relief even if completing after 1 June 2024. — [Moore Barlow](https://www.moorebarlow.com/blog/multiple-dwellings-relief-to-be-abolished-from-1-june-2024-your-key-questions-answered/); [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2024/03/multiple-dwellings-relief-abolished); [Deloitte TaxScape](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-spring-budget-2024/stamp-duty-land-tax-(sdlt)---multiple-dwellings-relief.aspx)
- **31 October 2024 [IN FORCE]:** Higher Rates for Additional Dwellings (the surcharge on second homes and buy-to-let, originally 3% from 2016) raised to 5%, for transactions with an effective date on or after 31 October 2024; contracts exchanged before that date were protected subject to transitional rules. — [Simpkins Edwards](https://www.simpkinsedwards.co.uk/articles/sdlt-higher-rate-for-additional-dwellings-increased); [Deloitte TaxScape](https://taxscape.deloitte.com/measures-autumn-budget-2024/stamp-duty-land-tax---higher-rates-for-additional-dwellings.aspx)
- **31 October 2024 [IN FORCE]:** The flat "enveloped" SDLT rate for non-natural persons (companies) buying dwellings rose from 15% to 17%. — [Practical Law (Thomson Reuters)](https://uk.practicallaw.thomsonreuters.com/w-044-5092?transitionType=Default&contextData=(sc.Default))
- **1 April 2025 [IN FORCE]:** thresholds reverted to pre-September-2022 levels. Standard residential bands: 0% to £125,000; 2% £125,001–£250,000; 5% £250,001–£925,000; 10% £925,001–£1.5m; 12% above £1.5m (surcharge-payers add 5 points to each band). — [Simpkins Edwards](https://www.simpkinsedwards.co.uk/articles/sdlt-higher-rate-for-additional-dwellings-increased); [Rayner Essex](https://rayneressex.com/news/stamp-duty-land-tax-rate-changes/)
- **Autumn Budget 2025 (November 2025) [ANNOUNCED, from April 2028]:** a High Value Council Tax Surcharge ("mansion tax") on English homes valued above £2m, in four bands from £2,500 a year up to £7,500 for homes above £5m, with five-yearly revaluations. This is a council-tax charge, not SDLT. — [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/autumn-budget-2025-property/); [Womble Bond Dickinson](https://www.womblebonddickinson.com/uk/insights/articles-and-briefings/autumn-budget-2025-tax-hikes-fiscal-drag-and-return-mansion-tax)

### Inferences
- **Who it hits:** the 5% surcharge hits every landlord (L) and flipper (F) buying an additional residential property. It hits flippers especially hard because it is paid on every purchase, and a quick resale leaves no rental years to spread it over. On a £250k flip, the surcharge alone is 5% × £250k = £12,500, on top of standard SDLT. Losing MDR hurts portfolio and block buyers (L), and developers or investors buying several units at once (D).
- **Behavioural effect:** investors want higher gross yields or deeper purchase discounts (below-market-value deals, auctions, distressed sellers) to absorb up-front tax. This sharpens demand for sourced below-market-value deals (DS). It also favours strategies that add value, such as conversions, HMOs and title splits, over "vanilla" buy-to-let. The prime-London "mansion tax" from 2028 is mainly a prime owner-occupier and prime-landlord issue, not a mass-market investor one.
- Timing spikes: each deadline (30 June 2021, 31 March 2025, 31 October 2024) probably pulled transactions forward. That is my inference; I did not source transaction data.

### Gaps
- Could not open HMRC/GOV.UK to confirm whether the November 2025 Budget or any 2026 fiscal event changed SDLT rates. Search results were silent on SDLT changes in Budget 2025, which suggests none, but this is unverified.
- The date of the Autumn Budget 2026 and any pre-Budget SDLT or property-tax proposals (e.g., replacing SDLT with an annual property tax) were not researched (search budget exhausted).
- Exact first-time-buyer thresholds (believed to be £425k/£625k to £300k/£500k on 1 April 2025) were not confirmed in a source this session.
- Company buyers pay the surcharge even on their first dwelling. The 17% rate usually does not apply to genuine rental businesses, which can claim relief. I know this but did not source it this session.

## 2. Income tax, Section 24, the shift to limited companies, CGT and incorporation relief

### Takeaway
Since Section 24 (phased in from 2017 to 2020) stopped individual landlords from deducting mortgage interest (replacing it with a basic-rate tax credit), new buy-to-let buying has moved heavily into limited companies. About three-quarters of new purchases were company-owned by 2025, and a record 66,587 buy-to-let companies were formed that year. The November 2025 Budget widened the gap further: separate property-income tax rates of 22%/42%/47% from April 2027, and dividend tax up 2 points from April 2026. It also tightened incorporation relief, which now must be claimed. Residential CGT stayed at 18%/24% in October 2024 while other assets' CGT rose to match.

### Cited Findings
- **Section 24 [IN FORCE since phasing 2017 onwards]:** the tapering of mortgage-interest tax relief since 2017 ("Section 24") has pushed investors into companies; incorporated landlords can still deduct mortgage interest before tax. — [Hamptons](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/record-number-of-buy-to-let-companies-set-up-in-2025); [Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/record-number-of-buy-to-let-companies-set-up-in-2025-hamptons.html)
- **Shift to companies (data):** a record 66,587 buy-to-let companies were set up in 2025 (+8% on 2024; +363% over a decade), peaking at 6,493 in September 2025. About 443,272 buy-to-let companies were registered at Companies House by end-2025, almost five times the 2016 figure. — [Hamptons](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/record-number-of-buy-to-let-companies-set-up-in-2025); [Property Reporter](https://www.propertyreporter.co.uk/record-number-of-buy-to-let-companies-set-up-in-2025-hamptons.html)
- Share of new buy-to-let purchases made through a company: around 10% in 2016, about 40% in 2022, and "around three-quarters" by 2025. — [Hamptons (2022 index)](https://hamptons.co.uk/research/articles/september-22-rental-index); [Hamptons (2025)](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/record-number-of-buy-to-let-companies-set-up-in-2025); [Property Investor Today](https://www.propertyinvestortoday.co.uk/breaking-news/2025/03/buy-to-let-investors-set-up-record-number-of-limited-companies/) (note: Hamptons figures, attributed via search summaries)
- **CGT, 30 October 2024 [IN FORCE]:** main CGT rates rose immediately from 10% to 18% and from 20% to 24%; residential property CGT rates stayed at 18%/24%, so the rates are now aligned. — [Michelmores](https://www.michelmores.com/tax-insight/autumn-budget-2024-capital-gains-tax/); [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/autumn-budget-2024-capital-gains-tax/)
- **Furnished Holiday Lettings regime abolished, April 2025 [IN FORCE]:** holiday-let income is now taxed as ordinary property income, like buy-to-let, losing the trading-style reliefs. — [August](https://www.augustapp.com/blog/holiday-let-tax-changes); [Tide](https://www.tide.co/blog/tax/making-tax-digital-landlords-guide/)
- **Incorporation relief, from 6 April 2026 [IN FORCE]:** CGT incorporation relief (moving a property business into a company in exchange for shares) no longer applies automatically. It must be claimed in the transferor's self-assessment return with details of the transaction, the computations and the business type, which gives HMRC visibility to challenge claims. — [PKF Francis Clark](https://pkf-francisclark.co.uk/insights/budget-tax-changes-affecting-property-dividends-and-savings-income/); [Price Bailey](https://www.pricebailey.co.uk/blog/incorporation-relief/); [Mosaic Chambers](https://www.mosaicchambers.com/2025-budget-incorporation-relief)
- **Dividend tax, from 6 April 2026 [IN FORCE]:** basic and higher dividend rates up 2 points, to 10.75% and 35.75%; the additional rate stays at 39.35%. — [Saffery](https://www.saffery.com/insights/articles/autumn-budget-2025-at-a-glance-summary/); [naail & co](https://naailandco.com/budget-2025-dividend-tax-up-2-from-april-2026/)
- **Property income tax rates, from April 2027 [LEGISLATED/CONFIRMED, FUTURE DATE]:** rental income gets its own rates 2 points above standard rates (22%/42%/47%). Finance-cost relief (the Section 24 credit) will be given at the 22% basic property rate. Salary and other earned income stay on standard rates. — [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/news/budget-2025-reaction); [DJH](https://www.djh.co.uk/latest-news/news-insights/autumn-budget-2025-what-the-property-tax-changes-mean-for-landlords-and-property-owners/); [Evelyn Partners](https://www.evelyn.com/press-centre/all-press-releases/budget-2025-income-tax-on-savings-dividends-and-property-hiked/)
- **Making Tax Digital for Income Tax, from 6 April 2026 [IN FORCE]:** landlords and sole traders with qualifying (gross) income above £50,000 in 2024–25 must keep digital records and file quarterly updates. The threshold drops to £30,000 from 6 April 2027. — [Tide](https://www.tide.co/blog/tax/making-tax-digital-landlords-guide/); [Sage](https://www.sage.com/en-gb/blog/mtd-income-tax-landlords-need-to-know/)
- Commentators note incorporation may again be considered because of the income tax / corporation tax gap, but it is complex and may not suit landlords who need to extract significant profits. — [PKF Francis Clark](https://pkf-francisclark.co.uk/insights/budget-tax-changes-affecting-property-dividends-and-savings-income/)

### Inferences
- **Who it hits:** individual higher-rate landlords with mortgages (L) are hit hardest by Section 24. The April 2027 property income rates hit every individual landlord, mortgaged or not. The 2026 dividend rise and the incorporation-relief claim rule partly offset the company advantage. Flippers who trade through companies (F) are mainly hit by the dividend rise when they extract profit. Flippers trading personally are taxed as traders on income, not CGT (background principle, not sourced here).
- **Behavioural effect:** (1) new purchases are made almost only in special-purpose-vehicle (SPV) companies; (2) existing individual landlords face a "sell, incorporate or hold" choice, and the 2026 claim requirement and SDLT on transfers make incorporation harder; (3) investors favour higher-yield, lower-leverage and value-add strategies (HMOs, serviced accommodation, conversions) that can carry the tax; (4) there is more demand for accountants, SPV-friendly lenders and software (MTD).
- Unmortgaged individual landlords were largely untouched by Section 24 but *are* hit by the 2027 property rates. That broadens the push to sell or restructure.

### Gaps
- Could not source the Section 24 mechanics directly (Finance (No. 2) Act 2015; phased 25%/50%/75%/100% over 2017–18 to 2020–21; 20% basic-rate credit). This is my background knowledge, not verified this session.
- Could not verify the Spring Budget 2024 cut in the higher residential CGT rate from 28% to 24% (effective 6 April 2024). Sources confirm only that the rate was 24% and "unchanged" in October 2024.
- The MTD threshold after 2027 (believed £20,000 from April 2028) was not confirmed.
- No reliable data found on how many landlords actually sold because of tax changes, or on rental stock trends 2020–2026 (search budget exhausted).

## 3. Renters' Rights Act 2025, HMO licensing, Article 4 directions and selective licensing

### Takeaway
The Renters' Rights Act 2025 (Royal Assent 27 October 2025) abolished Section 21 "no-fault" evictions and fixed-term assured shorthold tenancies (ASTs) from 1 May 2026. All new private lets are now open-ended assured periodic tenancies, rent rises are limited to once a year, and sale or move-in possession is barred in the first 12 months with 4 months' notice. A national landlord database rolls out from late 2026 and a mandatory ombudsman is expected around 2028. Separately, since 23 December 2024 councils can impose borough-wide selective licensing without ministerial sign-off.

### Cited Findings
- **27 October 2025:** Renters' Rights Act 2025 received Royal Assent. — [UK Parliament Bills](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/3764/stages/20254); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters'_Rights_Act_2025)
- Policy context: private rents in England rose 8.6% in the year to July 2024 (London 9.7%); the Act aims to "dampen rent rises". — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters'_Rights_Act_2025)
- **1 May 2026 [IN FORCE] (Phase 1):** Section 21 notices and ASTs in the private rented sector abolished; every new private tenancy is automatically an assured (periodic) tenancy; landlords must use Section 8 grounds to regain possession. — [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/abolition-of-section-21-notices-from-1-may-2026-key-practical-tips-to-avoid-procedural-risks/); [Shelter England](https://england.shelter.org.uk/professional_resources/news_and_updates/how_section_21_notices_will_be_abolished)
- **Transitional dates:** 30 April 2026 was the last day to serve a valid Section 21 notice; 31 July 2026 was the last day to issue a Section 21 possession claim at court. — [Osbornes Law](https://osborneslaw.com/blog/section-21-abolition-key-dates/); [Kennedys](https://www.kennedyslaw.com/en/thought-leadership/article/abolition-of-section-21-notices-from-1-may-2026-key-practical-tips-to-avoid-procedural-risks/)
- **Rent increases [IN FORCE from 1 May 2026]:** for periodic assured tenancies, increases must use the Section 13 (Housing Act 1988) process, once a year, with notice extended from one month to two months; tenants can challenge at tribunal. — [Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/your-guide-to-section-13-and-rent-increases-in-the-private-rented-sector); [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/resources/managing-your-tenancy/rent-increases-assured-tenancies)
- **Selling / moving in [IN FORCE]:** new mandatory Ground 1A (sale) and Ground 1 (landlord/family moving in) need four months' notice and cannot be used to make a tenant leave within the first 12 months of a tenancy. — [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/news/selling-under-the-renters-rights-act); [Keystone Law](https://keystonelaw.com/keynotes/the-renters-rights-act-2025-what-are-the-grounds-for-possession-and-their-notice-periods/); [Martin & Co](https://www.martinco.com/guides/landlord/the-12-month-protected-period-selling-or-moving-into-a-let-property/)
- **Rent in advance [IN FORCE]:** for tenancies signed on or after 1 May 2026, landlords cannot require payment ahead of a rental period once tenants have moved in; pre-May-2026 arrangements continue until those tenancies end. — [NRLA](https://www.nrla.org.uk/resources/renters-rights/existing-tenancies-renters-rights-act)
- **Rental bidding banned [IN FORCE]:** landlords and agents must publish an asking rent and may not ask for, encourage or accept bids above it. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters'_Rights_Act_2025); [GOV.UK guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)
- **Pets [IN FORCE]:** landlords cannot unreasonably refuse a tenant's request to keep a pet and must consider each request case by case. — [GOV.UK guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act)
- **Enforcement:** civil penalties up to £7,000 for initial or minor breaches and up to £40,000 for serious, persistent or repeat breaches, or criminal prosecution instead. — [GOV.UK guide](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act/guide-to-the-renters-rights-act); [Great Yarmouth BC](https://www.great-yarmouth.gov.uk/article/15575/Civil-penalties-under-the-Renters-Rights-Act-2025-and-other-housing-legislation)
- **Phase 2, PRS Database [CONFIRMED, date TBC: "from late 2026"]:** mandatory for all private landlords, rolled out area by area, with an annual fee to be confirmed. Required data include landlord contact details, full address, property type, bedrooms, number of households/residents, occupancy and furnishing. — [Coventry City Council](https://www.coventry.gov.uk/housing-enforcement/renters-rights-act/3); [Goodlord](https://blog.goodlord.co/renters-rights-bill-a-letting-agents-guide); [Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/updates/renters-rights-act-implementation-dates/)
- **Ombudsman [EXPECTED 2028]:** to follow the database; membership expected to be mandatory for all landlords in 2028. — [Ward Hadaway](https://www.wardhadaway.com/insights/updates/renters-rights-act-implementation-dates/); [Coodes Solicitors](https://coodes.co.uk/2026/07/more-change-on-the-way-what-landlords-need-to-know-as-phase-2-of-the-renters-rights-act-approaches/)
- **Phase 3 [PROPOSED/FUTURE]:** a Decent Homes Standard applied to private renting for the first time, Awaab's Law (hazard-repair deadlines) extended to private landlords, and higher energy-efficiency standards; roadmaps run to 2035. — [HLC](https://www.hlc.com/en/publications/renters-rights-act-implementation-roadmap-now-published); [Ashmore Residential](https://www.ashmoreresidential.com/ar-news/renters-rights-act-timetable-landlords/)
- **Selective licensing, 23 December 2024 [IN FORCE]:** the Selective Licensing General Approval 2024 removed the need for Secretary of State approval for schemes covering more than 20% of a council's area or private rented stock. Councils can now designate up to borough-wide on their own authority, subject to Housing Act 2004 Part 3 tests and at least 10 weeks' consultation. More landlords are expected to need licences as a result. — [GOV.UK General Approval (PDF)](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/675c57651857548bccbcf9f0/General_Approval_2024.pdf); [Stephensons](https://www.stephensons.co.uk/site/blog/professional-discipline-blog/selective-licensing-the-general-approval); [Cornerstone Barristers](https://cornerstonebarristers.com/new-horizons-for-selective-licensing-schemes/); [Commercial Trust](https://www.commercialtrust.co.uk/news/labour-changes-selective-licensing/)
- Lender angle: law firms flag the Act's implications for real-estate financing (security of tenure, possession grounds). — [Travers Smith](https://www.traverssmith.com/knowledge/knowledge-container/the-renters-rights-act-2025-and-real-estate-financing/)

### Inferences
- **Who it hits:** all landlords (L), especially small "accidental" landlords who relied on Section 21 as a low-cost exit and on fixed terms for certainty. Flippers who buy tenanted stock (F) are affected because they can no longer quickly get vacant possession: they must wait out the first 12 months and give 4 months' notice. HMO operators (L) face licensing costs plus the new tenure rules.
- **Behavioural effect:** landlords screen tenants more tightly, pay more attention to arrears management and seek rent guarantee insurance. Some smaller landlords exit, and sales of tenanted properties rise (a potential source of discounted deals for investors and sourcers). Professional and company landlords consolidate. Rents are set higher at the start of a tenancy, because increases are capped to one Section 13 increase a year. Larger licensing areas add cost and compliance work, favouring professional managers.
- The database and ombudsman create a register that could later be used to enforce EPC and Decent Homes rules, and to check tax compliance. That is my inference.

### Gaps
- Could not find sourced detail on HMO-specific changes (mandatory HMO licensing thresholds, Article 4 directions removing the permitted change from family homes (C3) to small HMOs (C4)), nor a count of councils with HMO Article 4 directions. The August 2026 NPPF's treatment of HMOs was also not found.
- The Decent Homes Standard compliance date for private rentals (roadmaps imply around 2035) and the Awaab's Law private-sector start date were not confirmed from a primary source.
- The PRS database fee level and exact launch date were not yet published per sources.
- No sourced data on landlord exits or rental supply after 1 May 2026.

## 4. EPC minimum energy efficiency standards (MEES) for rentals

### Takeaway
The government has confirmed that privately rented homes in England must reach the equivalent of EPC C by 1 October 2030 (EPC E remains the minimum until then). Landlords' spend is capped at £10,000 per property, lowered from £15,000 in the consultation. The deadline is still four years away, so this is confirmed policy, not yet a live obligation.

### Cited Findings
- **Consultation, 7 February to 2 May 2025:** "Improving the energy performance of privately rented homes" proposed raising the standard to EPC C-equivalent by 2030. — [Elmhurst Energy](https://www.elmhurstenergy.co.uk/blog/2025/02/07/epc-c-by-2030-government-consults-on-big-changes-for-private-rented-sector/); [Devonshires](https://insights.devonshires.com/post/102mfc7/government-publishes-response-to-the-consultation-on-raising-energy-efficiency-st)

## 5. Deal sourcing rules: estate agency work, AML supervision, redress, data protection

### Takeaway
Deal sourcing (finding deals and introducing them to investors for a fee) counts as "estate agency work" under section 1 of the Estate Agents Act 1979. A sourcer must therefore register with HMRC for anti-money-laundering (AML) supervision *before* trading (trading unregistered is a criminal offence), join an approved redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or the Property Redress Scheme), and register with the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). Enforcement is real: HMRC has fined sourcing firms, and one report found most sourcers breach AML rules.

### Cited Findings
- Estate Agents Act 1979: section 1 defines estate agency work; the Act governs conduct, and unfit persons can be banned. — [legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1979/38)
- The Consumers, Estate Agents and Redress Act 2007 amended the 1979 Act (Part 3), including the redress requirement for estate agents. — [legislation.gov.uk (CEARA 2007 s.53)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2007/17/section/53/data.html)
- Anyone sourcing property for clients is carrying out estate agency work under s.1 of the 1979 Act. They must register with HMRC for AML supervision, belong to a government-approved redress scheme (The Property Ombudsman or Property Redress Scheme), and register with the ICO. — [August](https://www.augustapp.com/dictionary/property-sourcing); [PropSourcer](https://www.propsourcer.com/sourcing-growth-column/is-property-sourcing-legal-in-the-uk-what-the-law-actually-says)
- HMRC is the AML supervisor for estate agency businesses. Businesses must register before carrying on estate agency activity and pay a registration fee and an annual supervision fee; trading while unregistered, or after cancellation, is a criminal offence. — [HMRC AMLG2200 (GOV.UK)](https://www.gov.uk/hmrc-internal-manuals/anti-money-laundering-guidance-for-supervised-businesses/amlg2200); [August](https://www.augustapp.com/dictionary/property-sourcing)
- HMRC has sent "nudge" letters to estate agents (including sourcers) to register for AML supervision. — [Ross Martin](https://www.rossmartin.co.uk/sme-tax-news/8210-estate-agents-receive-nudge-to-register-for-aml-supervision)
- A report found almost all property-sourcing agents breach AML rules. — [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/almost-all-property-sourcing-agents-breach-aml-rules/)
- 136 sourcing firms have been fined over £1.1 million in total by HMRC for failing to register for AML supervision. — reported in search summaries of [PropSourcer](https://www.propsourcer.com/sourcing-growth-column/is-property-sourcing-legal-in-the-uk-what-the-law-actually-says) / [The Negotiator](https://thenegotiator.co.uk/news/regulation-law-news/almost-all-property-sourcing-agents-breach-aml-rules/); exact source and period not verified.
- From 1 May 2026, the Renters' Rights Act ban on rental bidding also covers letting agents. That matters to sourcers who package rent-to-rent or let deals. — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renters'_Rights_Act_2025)

### Inferences
- **Who it hits:** deal sourcers (DS), and flippers or developers who sell sourced deals on to others. **Behavioural effect:** the barrier to entry for sourcing is low, but compliance (AML checks on both parties, redress scheme membership, ICO registration, professional indemnity insurance, clear fee terms) is now a differentiator. Investors increasingly ask for AML and redress registration numbers. A platform that verifies sourcer compliance addresses a known pain point.
- The 2020–26 changes do not create new sourcing law. They raise the stakes for sourcers through higher investor tax costs (demand for discounts), HMRC enforcement, and tenancy reform (vacant-possession deals become scarcer and more valuable).

### Gaps
- Current HMRC AML registration and annual fee amounts, and the ICO data-protection fee, were not confirmed.

## 7. Building safety, second staircases, Building Safety Levy and the Future Homes Standard

### Takeaway
Three build-cost shifts land in 2026–27. Second staircases are mandatory for new residential buildings over 18m from 30 September 2026. The Building Safety Levy (a per-m² charge on new homes to fund cladding remediation) starts 1 October 2026 for schemes of 10+ homes. In July 2026 the government dropped the proposed exemption for 10–49-home "medium" sites. The Future Homes Standard (published 24 March 2026, applying from 24 March 2027) effectively mandates heat pumps and solar PV on most new homes.

### Cited Findings
- **Second staircase [IN FORCE 30 September 2026]:** updated Approved Document B (published 29 March 2024) requires two staircases in new residential buildings over 18m from 30 September 2026, unless a project is "sufficiently progressed" by then. — [Forsters](https://www.forsters.co.uk/news-and-views/second-staircases-mandatory-in-residential-buildings-over-18m-from-30-september-2026); [Planning Geek](https://www.planninggeek.co.uk/2026/second-staircase/)

Developments of fewer than 10 units are exempt, as are affordable housing and various specialist uses. Previously developed (brownfield) land gets a 50% rate where at least 75% of the site is previously developed land. — [Taylor Wessing](https://www.taylorwessing.com/en/insights-and-events/insights/2025/05/uc-building-safety-levy-to-start-from-october-2026); [Browne Jacobson](https://www.brownejacobson.com/insights/building-safety-levy-october-2026-what-to-expect); [Anstey Horne](https://www.ansteyhorne.co.uk/news/building-safety-levy-2026); [PAD Magazine](https://www.padmagazine.co.uk/planning-developments-2/building-safety-levy-2026-rates-scope-and-developer-checklist/34082/); [FMB](https://www.fmb.org.uk/resource/building-safety-levy-what-small-builders-need-to-know.html)
- **Medium-site BSL exemption [PROPOSED Dec 2025, then DROPPED July 2026]:** in December 2025 MHCLG proposed exempting 10–49-home sites, since most are delivered by SMEs. ### Inferences
- **Who it hits:** small developers (D) building 10+ homes now pay BSL, and SME lobby groups lost the fight for the 10–49 exemption. Schemes of 9 or fewer are exempt, which may cause "bunching" of applications just under 10 units. Brownfield schemes get half-rate BSL. The FHS raises per-unit build costs for all new-build developers and self-builders (D, SB) from 2027. Developers may rush to get sites registered or started under the old Part L before the transition ends. The second staircase mainly affects taller flatted schemes (>18m, roughly 7+ storeys), which small developers rarely build, but it shapes what larger urban sites are worth.
- **Behavioural effect:** margins on small new-build schemes are squeezed (BSL + FHS + BNG for sites above 0.2 ha). This pushes SMEs toward sub-10-unit schemes, brownfield (BSL discount, likely BNG exemption), and conversions or permitted development, which are not new-build.

## 8. Interest-rate environment (Bank Rate 2020 to 2026) and effect on buy-to-let and bridging lending

### Takeaway
Bank Rate went from an emergency 0.1% (until December 2021) to 5.25% by August 2023 through 14 consecutive rises. It was then cut six times between August 2024 and 18 December 2025, to 3.75%. It has been held at 3.75% at every 2026 meeting to date (latest 16 September 2026), with a growing minority voting to *raise* to 4%. For investors, the era of cheap leverage that made thin-yield buy-to-let work is over. Buy-to-let rates peaked at about 6.8% (August 2023), and lenders repriced upward again in 2026 on expectations of no further cuts.
