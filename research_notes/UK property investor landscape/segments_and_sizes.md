## 1. Landlords: how many, portfolio sizes, limited companies, HMOs, selling vs buying, BTL mortgages

### Takeaway
Roughly 2.9m UK individuals declare rental income, and the number is now edging down (2.91m in 2023/24 to 2.88m in 2024/25). In England the landlord base is split: 45% of landlords own a single property, and the 17% with 5+ properties hold about half of all tenancies. Individual landlords are shrinking or selling, while limited-company landlords are growing (443k active BTL companies, a record 66.6k formed in 2025). That incorporation boom has stalled in 2026, the first annual fall since 2008 is on track, and after the Renters' Rights Act (1 May 2026) landlord purchases overtook landlord sales for the first time since 2019.

### Inferences
- EPLS 2024 split by subtraction: 45% of landlords own 1 property, about 38% own 2–4, and 17% own 5+. By tenancies: 21%, about 30% and 49%. A share for "4+" (the PRA "portfolio landlord" definition) was not captured.
- The HMRC count of about 2.88m is a UK-wide count of people with any property income on self-assessment, including holiday lets, commercial lets and jointly owned lets counted per person. It overstates the number of distinct residential landlord businesses and excludes companies. The 443k BTL companies sit on top of it, with overlap in the people behind them.
- The investor base is professionalising: individuals are shrinking slowly, companies grew about 5x in a decade, and 5+ landlords hold half of tenancies. Those professional operators are the most likely repeat users of a screening tool. The pool of new company formations (about 66k in 2025, trending about −8% in 2026) gives a rough yearly count of "newly serious" investors.
- BTL purchase mortgages ran at about 16,800 a quarter in Q1 2026, roughly 67k a year annualised. If landlords make 10–13% of about 1.1m transactions (about 110k–145k purchases a year), perhaps half or more of landlord purchases are cash, bridging or company-lender funded rather than standard BTL mortgages. This is a rough inference: the denominators differ (UK vs GB) and the data is incomplete.
- After the Renters' Rights Act, supply of tenanted ex-rental stock onto the sale market is falling (9.2% of listings, against 11.3%). Fewer tired ex-rentals may be available to value-add buyers in H2 2026 than in 2024–25.

### Gaps
- Official estimate of total private landlords in England. EPLS reports proportions, not a total. The commonly quoted "about 2.3m landlords in England" (MHCLG / Renters' Rights impact assessment) is **not verified** here because the search budget ran out.
- Exact EPLS 2024 share of landlords in limited companies (earlier EPLS rounds were about 5–7% in 2018; not verified), and share letting HMOs.
- Paragon / BVA BDRC data on portfolio landlords (4+ mortgaged properties) and average portfolio size: not retrieved.
- Full-year 2025 UK Finance BTL purchase-loan count and Q2 2026 UK Finance release: not retrieved.
- Stock of outstanding BTL mortgages (roughly 1.9–2.0m per UK Finance): not verified.
- England's new PRS landlord database (NRLA says rollout starts in December; year and details not verified), which will eventually give a hard landlord count.

## 2. Flippers: volume, profit, regions, types, auctions

### Takeaway
Flipping (buy and resell within 12 months) is a small niche that is shrinking. Hamptons counts about 10,570 flips in England & Wales in 2025, 1.5% of sales, half the 2016 peak of 21,560. Average gross profit after stamp duty fell 55% in a decade to about £16,390, mainly because of the 5% second-home SDLT surcharge. Activity has shifted to the cheap North and Midlands: the North East is the hotspot with about 4.7% of sales being flips, and London is the weakest.

### Cited Findings
- 2025: 10,570 homes flipped (resold within 12 months) in England & Wales, 1.5% of all transactions, against 2% in 2024. That compares with 21,560 in 2016. — [Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/); [The Intermediary, Apr 2026 "Share of flipped homes falls to decade low"](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2026/04/share-of-flipped-homes-falls-to-decade-low-hamptons-finds/)
- Average gross profit per flip after stamp duty was £16,390 in 2025, against £36,500 in 2015 (−55.1%). — [Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/)
- 73.3% of 2025 flips made a gross profit, falling to 58.7% after stamp duty. — [Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/)
- Stamp duty took 43% of the average flip's gross profit, about £12,400. The additional-dwelling surcharge rose from 3% to 5% in (Oct) 2024. — [Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/); [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/home-flipping-on-the-decline-as-profits-drop-hamptons/)
- Regional mix (Q1 2025): 61% of flips were in the Midlands, the North of England or Wales, up from 50% a decade earlier. — [Hamptons Flipping index Q1 2025](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/flipping-index-q1-2025)
- North East (Q1 2025): 4.7% of homes sold had been bought within the previous 12 months, more than double the national average. Average return was 36.4% (27% in 2015). It is the only English region where the share of profit left after stamp duty has risen, and SDLT is only about £6,000 per flip. — [Hamptons Flipping index Q1 2025](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/flipping-index-q1-2025); [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/home-flipping-on-the-decline-as-profits-drop-hamptons/)
- London (Q1 2025): the lowest region, with 1.5% of homes sold being flips, down from 3.2% a decade earlier. Average investor return is expected to fall to about 8% after about £33,000 of stamp duty per flip. — [Hamptons Flipping index Q1 2025](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/flipping-index-q1-2025); [Mortgage Strategy](https://www.mortgagestrategy.co.uk/news/home-flipping-on-the-decline-as-profits-drop-hamptons/)
- Consumer and press coverage of flipping hotspots and alternatives exists. — [MoneyWeek](https://moneyweek.com/investments/property/flipping-houses-hotspots-and-alternatives); [Yahoo Finance UK](https://uk.finance.yahoo.com/news/home-flipping-hotspots-where-properties-230100239.html)
- *Consistency flag:* a national 2025 share of 1.5% sits oddly next to London being "bottom" at 1.5% in Q1 2025. The quarterly and annual series probably use different definitions or periods. Use the annual figure (10,570, 1.5%) as the headline.

### Inferences
- 10,570 flips at 1.5% implies a denominator of about 700k sales. So Hamptons uses Land Registry-registered E&W sales, not HMRC's UK count of about 1.1m transactions (which includes Scotland and NI and non-market transfers). Don't mix the two.
- Pure 12-month flips are about 10k deals a year nationally, a small market for a tool on its own. The larger addressable group is landlords and small builders doing value-add or BRRR (buy, refurbish, refinance, rent) and holding longer. Hamptons' 12-month definition misses these.
- Economics favour low-price, low-SDLT areas (NE: about £6k SDLT, 36% returns; London: about £33k SDLT, about 8% returns). Regional price bands and SDLT drag should be first-order screening variables.

### Gaps
- No data found on flip **types** (cosmetic vs full refurb vs extension vs conversion vs title split or planning gain). Hamptons publishes counts and profit only. I found no UK source quantifying title splits or HMO conversions as a share of flips.
- No 2026 year-to-date flipping figures retrieved.
- **Auction purchases by investors**: not retrieved (search budget exhausted). Leads to follow: Essential Information Group (EIG) annual auction statistics (lots offered and sold, sale rates) and Auction House UK annual reports. Investor share of auction buyers was not found.
- Average holding period, refurb spend and financing (bridging-loan volumes, e.g. from the Bridging Trends / ASTL series) not retrieved.

## 3. SME and small housebuilders

### Takeaway
SME housebuilders have collapsed over three decades. More than 12,000 SMEs built about 40% of new homes in 1988, against about 2,500 building about 12% by 2017 (HBF; older data, still the standard reference). The FMB's 2025 survey names planning (47%), material costs (41%) and lack of sites (33%) as the top barriers. 60% say access to small sites is poor, and two-thirds drop sites because of expected Section 106 costs.

### Cited Findings
- In 1988, more than 12,000 SME builders delivered about 40% of new homes. By 2017 about 2,500 SMEs delivered about 12%. Getting back to 2007 firm numbers could add about 25,000 homes a year. *Older data (HBF, "Reversing the decline of small housebuilders", 2017).* — [The Construction Index, "HBF makes case for SME house-builders"](https://www.theconstructionindex.co.uk/news/view/hbf-makes-case-for-sme-house-builders); [United Trust Bank](https://www.utbank.co.uk/wise-owls/reversing-the-decline-of-sme-home-builders/); [HBF written evidence UKH0044](https://committees.parliament.uk/writtenevidence/38895/html/)
- A later HBF report argues that supporting SME housebuilders could unlock 100,000 homes (headline figure; date and method not retrieved). — [PBC Today](https://www.pbctoday.co.uk/news/planning-construction-news/hbf-report-says-sme-housebuilder-support-could-unlock-100000-homes/156702)
- The House of Lords Built Environment Committee has a chapter on SME housebuilders. — [Lords Built Environment Committee, Ch. 4](https://publications.parliament.uk/pa/ld5802/ldselect/ldbuiltenv/132/13208.htm)

## 4. Self-builders and custom-build

### Takeaway
Demand registered through Right to Build registers in England is at a record but plateauing: over 63,000 people as of April 2023, with the Oct 2024 data showing a new record but new sign-ups roughly offset by removals. Completions are far lower. The best proxy found is about 15,900 single-dwelling completions in 2019 (older data).

### Cited Findings
- Right to Build registers (year to Oct 2024): registrations reached a record, but numbers are plateauing as new registrants are offset by removals. 39% of local authorities had adopted percentage policies (plots reserved on large sites), up from 34% the year before. Only 123 councils said they tell registrants about opportunities, against 166 in 2018. — [NaCSBA "Right to Build Registers Data"](https://nacsba.org.uk/blog/right-to-build-registers-data/)
- Over 63,000 people were on custom and self-build registers in England as of April 2023. An earlier NaCSBA estimate was about 55,000. *Older data.* — [NaCSBA self-build registers](https://nacsba.org.uk/news/self-build-registers/); [NaCSBA register data](https://nacsba.org.uk/news/register-data/)
- NaCSBA also reports falling planning permissions granted for custom and self-build in some years, and alleges that some councils use "dirty tricks" to shrink their registers. — [NaCSBA register data](https://nacsba.org.uk/news/register-data/); [NaCSBA self-build registers](https://nacsba.org.uk/news/self-build-registers/)
- 15,902 single dwellings were completed in 2019, a proxy for self-build and custom-build output. *Older data.* — [NaCSBA single-dwelling data](https://nacsba.org.uk/news/single-dwelling-data-shows-self-build-numbers-growing/)
- An evidence review on self-build and custom housebuilding was published by Cambridge Land Economy (May 2024). — [Cambridge Land Economy](https://www.landecon.cam.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2024-05/self%20build%20rapid%20review%20report.pdf); see also [BuildStore VAT survey](https://www.buildstore.co.uk/about-us/news/self-and-custom-build-vat-survey)

### Inferences
- Tens of thousands of registrants against low-teens-thousands of completions suggests plot supply, not demand, is the constraint. Self-builders are a plausible secondary audience for a tool that flags replacement-dwelling or large-plot opportunities.

### Gaps
- Exact register totals for Oct 2024 and Oct 2025 (the government monitoring data) were not retrieved. gov.uk was blocked and the search budget ran out.
- No 2024–2026 completions estimate. NaCSBA's long-quoted "about 13,000–15,000 a year" range was not verified.

## 7. Total market size: transactions and investor share of purchases

### Takeaway
The UK residential market runs at about 1.1m transactions a year (2024 confirmed; 2025 similar). Landlords make roughly 10–13% of GB purchases (13.3% in Jan–Apr 2026, a decade high, easing to 10.2% by June 2026), much of it trading between landlords. Flippers are about 1.5% of E&W sales. Plausibly 110k–145k investor purchases a year, of which only about 10k are 12-month flips.

### Cited Findings
- 2024: about 1.1m UK residential transactions at an average price of £343,822 (Savills market-size analysis). — [Savills, "UK housing market returned to growth in 2024"](https://www.savills.co.uk/insight-and-opinion/savills-news/374618/uk-housing-market-returned-to-growth-in-2024---increasing-by-%C2%A322.3bn-in-size)
- 2025: transactions rose for a second year to about 1.1m. *The exact HMRC full-year figure was not captured.* — [BuyAssociation "Despite budget wobbles, property transactions held steady in 2025"](https://www.buyassociationgroup.com/en-us/news/despite-budget-wobbles-property-transactions-held-steady-in-2025/); [HMRC monthly property transactions](https://www.gov.uk/government/statistics/monthly-property-transactions-completed-in-the-uk-with-value-40000-or-above)
- 2025 monthly (HMRC):
  - Jan 2025: 81,360 (not seasonally adjusted), +21% year on year.
  - Feb 2025: 108,250 (seasonally adjusted), +28%.
  - Sep 2025: 95,980 (seasonally adjusted), +4%.
  - Completions were pulled forward into March 2025 ahead of the 1 April 2025 SDLT threshold changes, leaving April and May weak.
  — [Mortgage Solutions, 28 Feb 2025](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2025/02/28/residential-property-transactions-up-14-year-on-year-hmrc/); [The Intermediary, Oct 2025](https://theintermediary.co.uk/2025/10/residential-property-transactions-see-4-annual-rise-in-september-hmrc/); [HMRC commentary](https://gov.uk/government/publications/monthly-property-transactions-completed-in-the-uk-with-value-40000-or-above/uk-monthly-property-transactions-commentary)
- Landlord share of purchases (GB, Hamptons):
  - 13.3% in Jan–Apr 2026, the highest since early 2016.
  - In London, the South East, South West and East: 9.1%, against 8.8% in 2025.
  - 10.2% in June 2026.
  — [Hamptons](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/investor-purchases-rise-as-landlords-buy-from-other-landlords); [Hamptons](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/landlord-sales-slow-following-the-renters-rights-act); [Mortgage Solutions, 18 May 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/05/18/rents-rise-as-landlords-consolidate-hamptons/)
- Landlord share of homes for sale: 9.2% of listings were ex-rentals in June 2026, against 11.3% a year earlier. — [Hamptons](https://www.hamptons.co.uk/articles/landlord-sales-slow-following-the-renters-rights-act)
- Flips: 10,570, 1.5% of E&W sales (2025). — [Mortgage Solutions, 13 Apr 2026](https://www.mortgagesolutions.co.uk/mortgage-news/2026/04/13/house-flipping-falls-to-low-as-property-taxes-eat-into-profits-hamptons/)

### Inferences

**Segment snapshot.** Figures come from the sources cited above. Derived numbers are marked "≈" and are my arithmetic.

| Segment | Size (latest) | Trend 2023→2026 |
|---|---|---|
| Individual landlords (UK, HMRC) | 2.88m (2024/25) | Slight decline from 2.91m peak (2023/24) |
| Single-property landlords (England, EPLS) | 45% of landlords, 21% of tenancies (2024) | Most likely to exit (38% expect to be out by end-2026) |
| 5+ property landlords (England, EPLS) | 17% of landlords, 49% of tenancies (2024) | Consolidating: buying from exiting landlords |
| BTL limited companies | 443,272 active (end-2025); 66,587 new in 2025 | Record 2025; −8% Jan–Aug 2026, first fall since 2008 on track |
| HMOs (England) | ≈473k HMOs, ≈93k licensed (2023/24) | New licences −5.9% in 2024 |
| Landlord purchases | 10–13% of GB purchases (2026) ≈ 110k–145k a year if applied to ≈1.1m | Up in H1 2026, mostly landlord-to-landlord |

| Flippers (12-month resale, E&W) | 10,570 flips, 1.5% of sales (2025) | Down about 51% from 21,560 (2016); profit −55% since 2015 |
| SME housebuilders | ≈2,500 firms, ≈12% of new homes (2017, old) | Long-run decline from 12,000+ (1988) |

- Applying 10–13% to UK totals overstates slightly: Hamptons measures GB, and HMRC counts include non-market transfers. Treat about 110k–145k landlord purchases a year as an order of magnitude only.
- The market for "screen houses for value-add potential" is bigger than flipping alone. It covers about 10k pure flips a year, a larger (unquantified) group of BRRR, refurbish-to-let and HMO-conversion buyers among the 110k+ landlord purchases, the SME builders chasing small sites, and self-builders seeking plots.
- 2026 headwinds for the core user base:
  - The 5% SDLT surcharge squeezes flip margins.
  - BTL purchase mortgage demand is falling.
  - The incorporation boom is fading.
  - The Renters' Rights Act makes vacant-possession exits slower.
- The tailwind: consolidation by professional and multi-property landlords, and pricing opportunities in the North and Midlands.

- Hamptons full-year 2025 landlord purchase share (believed to be about 10–11%; not verified) and the cash-buyer share of all purchases.
- Share of landlord purchases that need refurbishment or value-add work: no source found.
